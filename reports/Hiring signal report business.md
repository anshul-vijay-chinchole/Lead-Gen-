# Curation, not data, wins small recruitment agencies

A weekly Hiring Signal Report is a **viable small, cash-positive productised service** for a solo student founder, but **not a viable data business**. The raw product, fresh job postings plus a contact at the hiring company, is already sold to small agencies for £30 to $295 a month, so the founder can win only on what those products lack: single-niche curation, guaranteed week-over-week novelty, honest email labels and a done-for-you format, priced at about **$99–149 a month after a $49–79 paid pilot**. Demand is real: the UK has about 31,000 recruitment enterprises (roughly 80% micro), the US about 20,000–27,000 staffing and recruiting firms, and for the third year running Bullhorn's survey of about 2,300 firms names hiring freezes and falling job volumes as the biggest obstacles to winning new business. Value is not in doubt either, because one contingency placement earns about £7,000–£12,250 in the UK and an estimated $16,000–$24,000 on a US mid-level specialist role, so one extra filled job a year repays the report several times over. Timing is good but narrow: late 2026 is an early-recovery turning point, with growth concentrated in engineering and advanced manufacturing, construction and trades, and accounting and finance. The most urgent finding concerns the founder's own tool and must be fixed before the first paid delivery: the free Adzuna key behind the default playbook allows commercial use only as a 14-day trial, Apollo and Hunter forbid reselling their output, and named contacts bring GDPR Article 14 notice duties in the UK/EU and data-broker registration in California, so the current default data path cannot be sold as it stands. A realistic first 30 days yields one to three paid pilots from LinkedIn-first, hand-personalised outreach, which is enough to test the idea but not to replace a job.

> **Read this first: how far to trust these numbers.** Six researchers gathered this evidence on 2026-09-25, but the research environment's network proxy blocked direct page fetches for nearly every site (vendor pricing pages, SIA, REC, ICO, Adzuna, Apollo, Hunter, Reddit), and the shared search budget ran out part-way. **Every figure, price and provider term below comes from search-engine result extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Verify key figures and all provider terms on the live pages before relying on them**, above all Adzuna's, Apollo's, Hunter's and TheirStack's resale terms, competitor prices and the California registration fee. Many benchmarks come from vendors that sell to agencies (Bullhorn, Recruiterflow, Pin, Agency Leads, Instantly) and are flagged "vendor". Labels used throughout: a statement with an inline link is a **sourced fact** (as extracted); ***Inference*** marks the analysts' reasoning from those facts; ***Assumption*** marks an unsourced planning guess to be replaced with real data. **This is not legal advice**; a lawyer in each target market should review the legal section before any data about people is sold.

## About 40,000 small US and UK agencies compete for scarce job orders

The staffing industry is large, but most of its money sits outside the business this report would serve. SIA valued global staffing at **$620bn in 2024**, with the US the largest market at $184bn and the UK third at $50.8bn ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/forecast-estimates-us-staffing-industrys-cumulative-growth-by-2030)). US revenue shrank for three years to roughly $179bn in 2025, and SIA's September 2026 update projects **+2.4% to $183.1bn in 2026, the first growth year since 2022** ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/us-staffing-industry-forecast-september-2026-update)). The REC puts UK recruitment turnover at about **£42.9bn** and GVA at £40.6bn, but **76.7% of that GVA comes from temporary and contract placements**, and UK permanent placements fell from an estimated 806,400 in 2023 to **536,400 in 2024 (−33.5%)** ([REC RISR 2024/25](https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/research/recruitment-and-industry-status-report/uk-recruitment-industry-status-report-202425); [Kenect summary](https://www.kenectrecruitment.co.uk/insights/recruitment-industry-status-report-24-25/)). *Inference:* the permanent-fee business that buys client leads is roughly a quarter of UK sector value, on the order of £10bn of GVA, and a similar minority in the US, where ASA counts $124bn of 2024 sales as temp and contract ([ASA Playbook](https://www.asa-digital.net/amstaffingassoc/2025_staffing_industry_playbook/MobilePagedArticle.action?articleId=2101336)). Among other Tier-1 markets, Australia is about US$29.4bn, roughly the sixth-largest staffing market in the world ([Access Group, citing SIA](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-au/blog/rec-the-australian-recruitment-market-in-2026-what-the-latest-sia-data-means-for-recruitment-agencies/)); a Canadian figure of C$9.3bn is undated and low-confidence ([StaffMax](https://www.staffmax.ca/canadian-staffing-market-to-grow-2)).

The buyer pool is a long tail of very small firms. The REC counts **31,345 UK recruitment enterprises in 2025**, up from 29,635 in 2023 even as permanent placements collapsed ([REC](https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/research/recruitment-and-industry-status-report/uk-recruitment-industry-status-report-202425)), and 2022 data show **79.5% were micro-businesses with fewer than 10 staff** while 82% turned over less than £1m ([New Millennia, citing REC](https://www.newmillennia.co.uk/blog/12-recruitment-agency-statistics-you-need-to-know/)). US counts disagree by definition and year: IBISWorld lists **19,783** employment and recruiting agencies in 2025 on an internally inconsistent page ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/employment-recruiting-agencies/1463/)), while ASA cites **about 27,000 staffing and recruiting companies operating about 54,000 offices**, a figure dated 2021 ([ASA](https://americanstaffing.net/research/fact-sheets-analysis-staffing-industry-trends/staffing-industry-statistics/)). *Inference:* applying the UK micro share gives roughly 24,000–25,000 UK micro agencies and perhaps 15,000–22,000 small US agencies, about 40,000–47,000 potential buyers before any niche filter. No source counts agencies by specialism, so the buyer pool for any single niche is unknown. The largest global firms hold about 40% of revenue ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/largest-global-staffing-firms-represent-40-of-market)), which leaves the specialist tail buying tools one owner at a time; ATLAS's 2026 survey of more than 1,000 agency recruiters found **41.1% work "full-desk"**, meaning the same person wins clients and fills roles ([ATLAS, vendor](https://recruitwithatlas.com/resources/agency-recruitment-report-2026/)).

Winning clients is these owners' dominant problem. Bullhorn's GRID 2026 survey of about 2,300 recruitment professionals reports that for a third year **"tight talent pools and falling job volumes"** are firms' top challenges, that **hiring freezes and budget constraints are the biggest obstacles to winning new business**, and that **competition from other firms is now the second-biggest obstacle and growing** ([Bullhorn GRID 2026, vendor](https://www.bullhorn.com/grid/2026-industry-trends/report/)). The 2025 edition found **77% of firms focused on growing market share by winning new clients** ([Bullhorn GRID 2025, vendor](https://www.bullhorn.com/grid/2025-industry-trends/)), although Pin cites the same survey as saying only 40% made it their top priority ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/how-to-get-clients-staffing-agency/)), so the exact share is uncertain. Sentiment is improving (56% of GRID 2026 respondents grew revenue in 2025, against 40% a year earlier), but this is a vendor's sample, probably tilted toward healthier firms that adopt technology.

Agencies win work by phone, LinkedIn and email sequences, referrals, candidate-led marketing (pitching a strong candidate before a vacancy exists) and vacancy-led business development, which means calling companies that have just advertised ([ATLAS](https://recruitwithatlas.com/blog/recruitment-business-development/); [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/how-to-get-clients-staffing-agency/)). Vendor evidence consistently says a trigger improves outreach: Recruiterflow reports about **14% response to outreach timed to a leadership change versus 1.2% for a cold call** ([Recruiterflow](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/trigger-based-recruitment-bd/)), and Pin quotes Autobound's **18% response to job-posting-based outreach versus a 3.4% baseline** ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/how-to-get-clients-staffing-agency/)). Two findings pull the other way. Recruit Signals argues that reactive, vacancy-led selling worked when vacancies were plentiful "but that market is gone", because every agency sees the same public ad ([Recruit Signals](https://recruitsignals.com/intelligence/articles/recruitment-agency-bd-playbook-stabilising-market)), and Recruiterflow's platform data show **71% of placements come from candidates already in the agency's CRM** ([Recruiterflow](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/recruiting-benchmarks/)). *Inference:* a posting is worth most to an agency that already holds matching candidates, so a generic "who's hiring" list is worth little, and the real product is a niche-matched list that arrives before rivals act on it.

## One placement repays years of subscription; conversion is the unknown

Placement fees make the value case easy. Contingency fees cluster at **15–25% of first-year salary, with about 20% the most-cited benchmark** ([Manatal](https://www.manatal.com/blog/recruitment-fees)); UK guides put standard roles at 15–20% and specialist roles at 20–30%, with a worked example of **£7,000 on a £40,000 salary and £12,250 on £70,000 at 17.5%** ([On3works](https://on3works.com/resources/recruitment-agency-fees-uk); [Recruitly](https://recruitly.io/resources/recruitment-agency-fees-2026)). Retained search runs 25–35% of first-year pay, usually paid in thirds ([KiTalent](https://kitalent.com/executive-search-fees)). Productivity per recruiter is low in absolute terms: Recruiterflow's data on more than 2,100 firms show **5.21 placements per recruiter per year for the top quarter of firms against 1.38 for everyone else**, about seven submissions per hire, and mid-level permanent roles filled in 28–35 days ([Recruiterflow benchmarks](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/recruiting-benchmarks/); [Recruiterflow time-to-fill](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/time-to-fill-benchmarks/); [Hunt Scanlon](https://huntscanlon.com/what-the-top-25-percent-of-search-firms-do-differently-and-why-its-not-about-sourcing/)). *Inference:* when a typical recruiter makes one or two placements a year, each extra placement is a large share of a small firm's revenue.

The unknown is how often a lead becomes a filled job. No authoritative fill-rate survey exists and the two sources found conflict: a low-quality aggregator claims contingency desks fill 40–55% of job orders ([BusinessDojo](https://dojobusiness.com/blogs/news/recruitment-fill-rate)), while a vendor KPI guide treats a fall from 25% to 10% as a red flag, implying healthy rates near 20–25% ([Wiggli](https://www.wiggli.io/blog/6-metrics-recruitment-agencies-should-lookout-for-and-how-to-rescue-them)). The table turns those ranges into the value of one extra job order.

| Quantity | UK | US | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fee per placement | £7,000–£12,250 | $16,000–$24,000 | UK: sourced worked example at 17.5%. US: *Assumption* of $80k–$120k salary × 20% |
| Chance a contingency job order is filled | 20–55% | 25–50% | Conflicting vendor and low-quality sources |
| Expected fee value of one extra job order | about £1,400–£6,700 | about $4,000–$12,000 | *Inference*: fee × chance of filling |
| One year of the recommended report at $149/month | $1,788 | $1,788 | Recommendation (pricing section) |

*Inference (assuming £1 ≈ $1.30):* one extra placement a year repays the recommended price roughly five to thirteen times, and even one extra job order at its expected value covers a year's subscription one to five times in the UK and two to seven times in the US. Price is therefore not what stops a purchase; credibility and conversion are. No independent study measures the chain from posting to sales call to job order to placement, and the funnel percentages in the repository's own plan (`CONTEXT.md` section 5.5.3) are labelled guesses that should stay that way until pilot clients report outcomes. Buyers are also financially fragile: 35% of UK recruitment firms reported bad debt in the past year, against 29% across all sectors ([REC](https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/research/recruitment-and-industry-status-report/uk-recruitment-industry-status-report-202425)), and UK agency insolvencies rose to 413 in 2023/24, the third annual increase in a row ([Recruiter](https://www.recruiter.co.uk/news/2024/04/rise-insolvencies-recruitment-agencies)). *Inference:* monthly, cancel-anytime terms paid upfront suit both sides better than annual contracts.

## The late-2026 recovery favours engineering, trades and finance

US demand has turned, but narrowly. Indeed's US Job Postings Index reached **103.5 on 18 September 2026, up 0.7% year on year, the first positive annual reading in nearly four years**, with demand strong "particularly for engineers and in Personal Care & Home Health" while tech postings "remain depressed" ([Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/09/24/us-labor-market-snapshot-september-2026/)); software-development postings sat about **27.5% below pre-pandemic** in mid-2026 ([Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/23/us-labor-market-snapshot-june-2026/)). JOLTS showed **7.27M openings in July 2026, with manufacturing openings at their highest since December 2023**, yet hires slipped to 5.1M and the quit rate was 1.9%, a "low-hire, low-fire" market ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/u-job-openings-rise-7-173346381.html)). LinkUp's time-to-fill measure rose 6.2% to 47.73 days in Q1 2026 ([LinkUp](https://www.linkup.com/insights/blog/q1-2026-economic-indicator-report)). SIA's 2026 segment outlook ranks life sciences (+5%), engineering (+3%) and finance/accounting (+2%) ahead of IT and marketing (+1%) ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/us-staffing-industry-forecast-september-2026-update)), and Robert Half's permanent-placement revenue grew 2.5% (adjusted) in Q2 2026 ([Robert Half 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000315213/000031521326000040/rhiq22026ex991.htm)).

The UK lags. ONS vacancies fell to **702,000 in June–August 2026, the lowest in more than five years**, with vacancies at the smallest employers down 16.1% ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/bulletins/jobsandvacanciesintheuk/latest)), and Indeed UK postings were 31% below pre-pandemic in August ([Indeed Hiring Lab UK](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/uk/blog/2026/08/25/uk-labour-market-snapshot-august-2026/)). Yet the KPMG/REC permanent-placements index reached **50.0 in July and 50.5 in August 2026, its first rise since September 2022**, and permanent vacancies rose **only in Engineering and Accounting/Financial** ([KPMG/REC Sep 2026](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/09/kpmg-and-rec-uk-report-on-jobs-september-2026.html); [KPMG/REC Aug 2026](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/08/kpmg-and-rec-uk-report-on-jobs.html)). Listed agencies show the damage (Hays' FY26 permanent fees fell 12% like-for-like and PageGroup's UK gross profit fell 5.3% in Q2) ([Hays FY26](https://www.rns-pdf.londonstockexchange.com/rns/4022R_1-2026-8-19.pdf); [DirectorsTalk](https://www.directorstalkinterviews.com/pagegroup-q2-gross-profit-slips-0-2-as-americas-and-asia-grow/4121256043)), and the recovery is fragile: war in Iran and UK political turbulence caused the fastest fall in permanent recruitment for ten months in May 2026 ([KPMG/REC May 2026](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/05/kpmg-and-rec-uk-report-on-jobs.html)).

Three structural forces cut across every niche. AI lets employers screen and source in-house, which squeezes agencies that depend on volume placements ([Detroit News/Bloomberg](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/2026/02/18/ai-threatens-staffing-industry-as-companies-bring-recruitment-in-house/88738387007/)). Greenhouse estimates **18–22% of online postings are "ghost jobs"** that are not really being filled ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/11/ghost-job-postings-add-another-layer-of-uncertainty-to-stalled-jobs-picture.html)). And funding rounds now predict hiring poorly, since post-Series A hiring rates in 2024–25 ran below 2020–22 ([Revelio Labs](https://www.reveliolabs.com/news/tech/startups-are-hiring-less-and-raising-more)). *Inference:* raw postings must be checked before they are sold, and the right targets are hard-to-fill, specialist and senior roles, where AI cuts agencies out least and fees are highest.

Life sciences, SIA's fastest-growing segment, is a credible fourth niche but was not researched in depth. *Inference on region:* start in the US, which has the larger agency base, a visible demand upturn and (see the legal section) lighter notice duties for contacts outside California; add the UK second, in engineering and accounting/finance, once an Article 14 notice process exists; defer Canada and Australia. The timing window has a built-in expiry, because when hiring heats up agencies become short of candidates rather than clients, so positioning on hard-to-fill, long-open roles protects the product into 2027.

What that stack does not supply is the recurring labour of writing niche-specific searches, removing the same job when it recurs across boards and weeks, screening out stale and ghost postings, finding the actual hiring manager rather than the internal recruiter, and checking emails honestly. The cheapest contact data is contested, with rival vendors reporting 15–25% bounce rates and more than 500 G2 complaints about Apollo's accuracy against Apollo's own 97% claim ([Salesforge, competitor-authored](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/apollo-io-review)). *Inference on saturation:* the market is **highly saturated for generic "who's hiring plus contacts" data** and **moderately saturated for human-curated, single-niche weekly shortlists**. ## Charge $99–149 a month for curation, novelty and honesty

Buyers already know a price band of roughly $100–300 a month. Agency Leads charges $295 after a $95 first month (£219 in the UK on a six-month term), and its own content says verified staffing-lead databases "typically range from $200 to $800 per month" ([Agency Leads, vendor](https://agency-leads.com/recruitment-tools/best-lead-generation-tools-staffing-agencies-2026/)); JobGrabber and LeadsBrew sit at $99–299 and $99, and many owners already pay $89.99–119.99 for Sales Navigator Core ([LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/compare-plans)). Spacewalk's £30 and do-it-yourself stacks near $50 set the floor, while done-for-you selling at $2,000+ a month sets a ceiling small agencies do not reach. No survey measures what 1–20 recruiter agencies actually spend on business-development data each month, which is the largest gap in the evidence on willingness to pay.

*Inference (recommendation from those anchors):* sell a **four-week paid pilot at $49–79, paid upfront**, which copies Agency Leads' discounted first month and screens out prospects who will never pay. Follow it with a **$149/month list price** for one niche and one region, covering 15–25 hand-checked companies a week, each with job links, date first seen, days open, a "why now" line and the likely decision-maker where one can be lawfully sourced. Give the first three clients a **$99 founding price locked for six months** in exchange for feedback calls and a testimonial, and hold an **exclusive niche-plus-city tier at $249–299** until there is evidence that the report produces job orders. A 10–20% discount for three months paid upfront mirrors the 20–25% annual discounts agencies are used to from Apollo and Sales Navigator. Pricing higher fails because Agency Leads' $295 buys thousands of leads plus CRM export, so a student with no track record cannot charge more than the category leader; the repository's own suggested Starter price of $250–450 (`CONTEXT.md` section 5.6) sits at or above that anchor and is not supported at launch. Pricing lower fails because at $29–49 the report becomes indistinguishable from Job Radar or Apollo, and it must be sold as a service.

The differentiation the evidence supports is specific. Niche depth should rest on pain signals: Recruiterflow names "a role in your niche that's been posted for 45+ days without moving" as a conversion signal ([Recruiterflow](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/trigger-based-recruitment-bd/)), and vendor content calls first-time roles and clusters, such as three hires in one department within 30 days, the highest-converting opportunities ([Recruit Signals](https://recruitsignals.com/intelligence/articles/contact-hiring-managers-30-days-before-job-ads-appear-win)). "Never delivered twice" should be an explicit guarantee, since TheirStack and Coresignal remove duplicates *across sources* but no competitor was found promising no repeats *across deliveries* ([TheirStack](https://theirstack.com/en/our-data)); that is absence of evidence, not proof. Paired with a cap on subscribers per niche and region, the guarantee answers Bullhorn's finding that competition from other agencies is a growing obstacle. Honest email labels (verified, risky, guessed-unverified, not found) are the credible alternative to blanket "verified" claims in a category whose accuracy claims are contested and unaudited. Finally, the file should arrive as a finished shortlist with a "why now" line in a CSV that imports into Bullhorn or a similar CRM, since Agency Leads' Bullhorn sync shows buyers expect CRM import ([Agency Leads](https://agency-leads.com/integrations-marketplace/bullhorn-integration/)). The one-line answer to "we already use Agency Leads" follows: *they give you thousands of rows; this gives you twenty checked companies in your niche that you have never seen, who to call, and why now.*

## Adzuna, Apollo and Hunter terms block resale of the default stack

### Provider terms decide which sources can be sold

Adzuna's API terms let commercial organisations use the API only under a **14-day trial, strictly to validate coverage and quality**. The data may not be used "in its original format or in aggregation ... to deliver ongoing work or research without written consent", and every displayed advert needs a hyperlinked **"Jobs by Adzuna"** label of at least 116×23 pixels ([Adzuna API Terms](https://developer.adzuna.com/docs/terms_of_service)). *Inference:* a paid weekly report is ongoing commercial work, so the repository's default path needs a written Adzuna licence before the first paid delivery. Apollo licenses its service and API "solely for internal business purposes" and bars reselling or re-licensing its "Output Data" "in any manner or form" ([Apollo Terms](https://www.apollo.io/terms); [Apollo API Terms](https://www.apollo.io/terms/api)), and Hunter prohibits "reselling or otherwise distributing or disclosing" its data to third parties, including as a derived product or service ([Hunter Terms](https://www.hunter.io/terms-of-service)). *Inference:* putting either tool's output into a client file is exactly the redistribution both forbid, and using them to support the founder's own research is a grey area.

Public ATS job-board APIs (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby) need no authentication, exist so employers can publish postings, and expose no candidate data ([Greenhouse](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html); [Lever](https://github.com/lever/postings-api); [Ashby](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api)). No explicit resale licence was found for them. *Inference:* they carry low contract risk, but ad text may be the employer's copyright, so the safer delivery is facts plus a link (company, role, location, date first seen and the posting URL). Coresignal sells data under a separate signed agreement ([Coresignal](https://coresignal.com/terms-and-conditions/)). TheirStack publishes a guide for embedding its data in sales software, but its resale terms were not retrieved ([TheirStack](https://theirstack.com/en/docs/guides/sales-software-integration-guide)); one search result showed terms from StackScan, a different company, forbidding resale into lead databases, and those should not be attributed to TheirStack ([StackScan](https://www.stackscan.com/terms)). PredictLeads' redistribution terms were not found. Indeed bans automated access ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/legal)), and LinkedIn's User Agreement section 8.2, effective 3 November 2025, bans scripts, crawlers and "any other means" used to scrape or copy profiles and data ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)).

### Scraping case law matters less than privacy law

In the US, *Van Buren* (2021) narrowed the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act to a "gates-up-or-down" test ([Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2021/06/06/supreme-court-ends-long-running-circuit-split-over-cfaa-exceeds-authorized-access-issue-adopting-a-narrow-interpretation-that-will-reverberate-in-scraping-disputes-and-litigation-ov/)), and the Ninth Circuit held in 2022 that scraping public pages probably falls outside the Act ([Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/04/21/taking-cue-from-the-supreme-courts-van-buren-decision-ninth-circuit-releases-new-opinion-holding-scraping-of-publicly-available-website-data-falls-outside-of-cfaa/)). hiQ nevertheless lost on breach of contract and accepted a **$500,000 consent judgment** and an order to destroy its scraped data ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/linkedin-s-data-scraping-battle-hiq-labs-ends-proposed-judgment)). *Meta v. Bright Data* (January 2024) held that Meta's terms did not bind a scraper collecting data while logged off, but it is a single district-court ruling ([Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-meta-v-bright-data-significant-decision-for-web-scraping-industry/)), and Proxycurl, sued over fake-account scraping of LinkedIn, shut down its API on 4 July 2025 ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/)). In the UK, database right protects substantial investment in a database, and the Computer Misuse Act has never been tested on scraping ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2021/global/legal-weapons-in-the-fight-against-data-scraping)). Privacy regulators are the more likely threat. France's CNIL fined **Kaspr €240,000** in December 2024 for a LinkedIn-derived contact database, for failing to inform the people in it, for English-only notices and for five-year retention ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en/data-scraping-kaspr-fined-eu240000)); a 2025 EDPB item gives €200,000, and the conflict is unresolved ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2025/data-scraping-french-sa-fined-kaspr-eu200-000_en)). The UK Upper Tribunal held in October 2025 that UK GDPR reached US-based Clearview through a broad reading of "monitoring"; an appeal is pending ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2025/10/uk-upper-tribunal-hands-down-judgment-on-clearview-ai-inc/)). *Inference:* never scrape LinkedIn or Indeed for a product that is sold, and a founder outside the UK/EU who lists UK/EU people should assume GDPR applies.

### Named contacts trigger notice, registration and consent duties

Names, job titles and work emails are personal data in every target market. In the UK, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 confirms that direct marketing can be a legitimate interest ([LegalVision](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/data-use-access-act-2025/)); most provisions took effect on 5 February 2026 ([Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2026/02/key-aspects-of-the-data--use-and-access--act-take-effect.html)) and all data-protection provisions had commenced by 19 June 2026 ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/data-use-and-access-act-2025-duaa-compliance/)). Experian defeated most of the ICO's transparency enforcement notice at the Upper Tribunal in 2024 ([judgment](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukut/aac/2024/105)). A lawful basis therefore exists, but Article 14 still requires telling people whose data was not collected from them, within one month or at the first disclosure to a recipient if that comes sooner ([GDPR Art 14](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-14-gdpr/)). Poland's regulator rejected Bisnode's reliance on a website notice for millions of people it had not contacted, while it had emailed notices to about 682,000 others ([Blake Morgan](https://www.blakemorgan.co.uk/digital-marketing-agency-bisnode-fined-by-the-polish-dpa-for-failing-to-be-transparent-with-data-subjects/); [UODO](https://uodo.gov.pl/en/553/1572)). *Inference:* a founder holding each person's work email cannot credibly claim notice is disproportionate effort. On the client side, the UK's electronic-marketing rules (PECR) let clients email corporate subscribers such as limited companies and LLPs without consent, while sole traders and some partnerships need consent or the soft opt-in ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)), and maximum PECR fines are now £17.5m or 4% of turnover ([MFMac](https://www.mfmac.com/insights/data-protection/data-use-and-access-act-2025-increased-maximum-fines-for-cookies-and-direct-marketing-practices/)).

In the US, California's business-to-business exemption from the CCPA expired on 1 January 2023 ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2022/10/california-consumer-privacy-act-employee-and-b2b-exemptions-expire-january-1-2023)), and any business earning **50% or more of its revenue from selling personal information** is covered regardless of size ([CPPA](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html); [SecurePrivacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/ccpa-requirements-2026-complete-compliance-guide)). Selling data about people with whom you have no direct relationship makes you a **data broker**, which must register annually by 31 January ([CPPA](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers/)) for a 2026 fee reported as $6,000 by one source and $6,600 by another, with $200-a-day fines for not registering and a dedicated enforcement strike force ([Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/california-privacy-agency-launches-data-broker-strike-force-amid-delete-act-crackdown); [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/california-delete-act-drop-platform-data-brokers/)). **From 1 August 2026** registered brokers must check California's DROP deletion platform at least every 45 days and process its requests ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/drop-is-coming-due-what-californias-delete-act-means-for-data-brokers-in-august/)). Texas ($300) and Oregon ($600) also run broker registries, as does Vermont ([Monda](https://www.monda.ai/blog/data-broker-registries-in-the-us); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20231214-texas-and-oregon-adopt-new-rules-for-data-broker-laws)). *Inference:* a solo reseller of contact files almost certainly meets California's 50% test, so **leaving California residents out of delivered files is the legitimate cheap option at launch**; company-level signals for companies in those states are unaffected. In Canada, PIPEDA exempts business contact information only when used to communicate with someone about their job, and commentators say the exemption is lost for addresses **harvested or generated by software** ([McMillan](https://mcmillan.ca/insights/contact-information-posted-on-websites-not-necessarily-up-for-grabs/)); the anti-spam law (CASL) implies consent only for conspicuously published addresses ([CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm)). In Australia, a business that trades in personal information falls outside the Privacy Act's small-business exemption ([Artificer Legal](https://artificer.legal/changes-to-small-business-privacy-obligations)), and the Spam Act likewise relies on conspicuous publication ([Pointon Partners](https://pointonpartners.com.au/spam-act/)). *Inference:* the repository's default pattern-guessed emails (first.last@domain) resemble "generated" addresses and are unsuitable for Canadian and Australian recipients.

## LinkedIn works this week; cold email needs a month of warm-up

On a $100–200 budget the realistic channels are LinkedIn by hand from day one and low-volume, hand-written email; recruitment communities, directories and trainer partnerships were not researched and remain open leads. LinkedIn applies invitation limits to every member, restricts accounts for up to a month for too many unaccepted invitations, and may restrict accounts it suspects of automation ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/types-of-restrictions-for-sending-invitations)); unofficial guidance puts the limit at **about 100 invitations a week** ([Valley](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/linkedin-invitation-limit-in-2025-weekly-limits-more)). *Inference:* that allows about 400 personalised invitations a month, and automation tools are not worth risking the founder's main free channel.

Gmail's and Microsoft's bulk-sender rules start at **5,000 messages a day**. Gmail requires authentication and easy unsubscribes, wants spam rates below 0.10% and never at 0.30%, and has enforced more strictly since November 2025 ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en); [Google FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en)); Microsoft has rejected non-compliant bulk mail since May 2025 ([Microsoft](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%e2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%e2%80%90volume-senders/4399730)). A solo founder is far below that threshold, but complaints still decide whether mail arrives. New sending domains need **3–8 weeks of warm-up** (vendors disagree on the length) ([MailReach](https://www.mailreach.co/blog/how-to-warm-up-email-domain)); Google Workspace Business Starter costs $8.40 per user per month on the flexible plan ([Mailbird](https://www.getmailbird.com/google-workspace-pricing-small-business-guide/)), and Instantly Growth ($47) or Smartlead Basic ($39) adds more ([Cleanlist](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-09-01-instantly-pricing-guide); [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/smartlead-vs-instantly)). *Inference:* a full cold-email setup costs $70–95 in month one and cannot produce revenue in weeks one to three, so the cheaper route is 10–20 genuinely one-to-one emails a day from a single Workspace mailbox alongside LinkedIn. UK agencies are mostly limited companies that may receive business email without consent, but sole traders should be checked on Companies House first ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)).

Instantly's benchmark puts the average reply rate at **3.43%**, with top senders above 10%, **58% of replies arriving on the first email**, and the best campaigns under 80 words ([Instantly, vendor](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)); vendors selling signal-based selling claim 3–15% replies when a message opens with a real signal ([Execue, vendor](https://execue.io/blog/signal-based-lead-generation-recruitment-agencies)). *Inference:* the sample is the pitch. The first message should carry three to five real, fresh signals from the prospect's niche, while the full weekly list (more companies, contacts and context) stays the paid item, and interested prospects should move to a paid pilot rather than a free month, mirroring Agency Leads' $95 first month. Referrals carry weight with this audience, since vendor-cited Staffing Hub data show 86% of fast-growing agencies run formal referral programmes against 60% of non-growing ones ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/how-to-get-clients-staffing-agency/)), so every pilot client should be asked for one introduction.

## Conclusion

The legal constraints that rule out the easy path (reselling Adzuna jobs and Apollo or Hunter contacts) point to the same product the competitive gap rewards: a small number of hand-checked, niche-specific signals, with the decision-maker sourced from the company's own public footprint and labelled honestly. Anyone with $50 can match the data. What is hard to copy is a founder who knows one niche's employers week by week, refuses to repeat a lead, and tells clients plainly which emails are guesses. leadgen is therefore best treated as a productivity tool for a service business rather than as the product itself, and the business should be priced, capped and sold as a service.

Two implications emerge that no single finding shows. First, the product's value peaks now, while agencies are short of clients; by 2027 they may be short of candidates instead, so positioning should already lean toward hard-to-fill, long-open roles that stay valuable in a hot market. Second, the only durable answer to £30 feeds and $95 first months is evidence. The first client who can say "three of your leads became job orders" is worth more than any feature, so the pilot month exists above all to produce that sentence, publish it with permission, and use it to justify moving from $99 toward the $249–299 exclusive tier.
