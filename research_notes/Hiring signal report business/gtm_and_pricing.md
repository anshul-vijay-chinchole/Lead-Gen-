## Q1. Willingness to pay: what small agencies spend on BD data and tools, and what comparable "job leads for recruiters" products cost

### Takeaway
Tools that sell "companies that are hiring, plus hiring-manager contacts" to staffing and recruitment agencies already exist and cost roughly **$95–$300 a month (US) or about £219 a month (UK, 6-month commitment)**. Agencies also already pay for general prospecting tools such as Sales Navigator (about $90–$120 a month) and Apollo ($49–$149 per user per month). Done-for-you outsourced business development costs **$2,000–$12,000+ a month**. A curated weekly report for one niche sits between a self-serve database and an outsourced BD service. The evidence supports roughly **$99–$300 a month** as the range buyers already know. It doesn't support premium pricing at the start.

**Do agency owners value hiring triggers?**
- Recruitment-tech vendor guides tell agencies to "tie outreach to real hiring triggers: new job postings, funding announcements, and leadership changes". They say job boards "help recruitment agencies find new business and prospect new clients", that a niche "shortens sales cycles and commands higher fees", and that referrals are "the highest-converting client acquisition channel". *(Vendor content from ZoomInfo, Bullhorn, Leonar and others. ### Inferences
- **The price agencies are anchored to is about $100–$300 a month.** Self-serve databases built for exactly this job (Agency Leads, JobGrabber, LeadsBrew) cluster there, and Sales Navigator Core ($90–$120) is a line item many agency owners already carry. So a student founder's weekly niche report will most likely sell at **about $49–$99 for a first-month pilot, then $99–$199 a month**. Charging more than $300 a month means competing on outcomes with BD agencies ($2k+), which needs a track record the founder doesn't have yet.
- **The difference has to be curation, not volume.** Competitors sell "10,000+ leads a week" (Agency Leads). A 1–20 recruiter agency can't work that many. The founder's case is: *"20–50 pre-qualified companies in **your** niche this week, the right decision-maker for each, and why now."* That saves a recruiter's time rather than adding more data to sort through.
- **The substitution risk is real.** An agency owner with Apollo or Sales Navigator plus a job board can do this themselves, and TheirStack sells the raw signal from $49 a month. The report has to beat the time an owner or recruiter would spend building the list. That argues for pricing below roughly the cost of a few hours of a recruiter's time each week (inference; I didn't research recruiter pay rates).
- **Value anchor (not verified here):** contingent placement fees are commonly quoted at around 15–25% of first-year salary, so one extra placement would pay for years of a $99/month report. I couldn't verify a recruitment-specific fee benchmark in this pass (see Gaps). The only fee figure I found was for RPO ("25% fee of total job placement"), from a low-quality source ([GoCGO](https://www.gocgo.com/recruitment-process-outsourcing-pricing)), so the founder should not quote it.

- I found no survey data on **how much small agencies (1–20 recruiters) actually spend on BD data each month**. - I found no reliable, recruitment-specific figure for placement fees (US/UK contingent %) to anchor ROI claims.

## Q2. Channels to reach agency owners cheaply: LinkedIn, cold email and its deliverability rules, communities, directories and partnerships

### Takeaway
With a $100–$200 budget, the realistic channels are **LinkedIn (free: personalised connection requests at roughly 100 a week, plus DMs and posts) and low-volume cold email from 1–2 secondary domains**. Cold email costs about $60–$80 in the first month for domains, a couple of Workspace mailboxes and one sending tool, and needs **3–8 weeks of warm-up**. At solo-founder volumes (well under 5,000 emails a day), the Gmail/Yahoo/Microsoft "bulk sender" rules don't formally apply, but spam complaints still decide whether emails land. UK B2B cold email to limited companies and LLPs is legal without consent, but sole traders need consent. I couldn't gather evidence on communities, directories or trainer partnerships in this pass.

### Cited Findings
**LinkedIn**
- LinkedIn Help: **all members (Basic and Premium) are subject to invitation limits**. When you reach the limit, "you'll need to wait one week to start sending invitations again." — [LinkedIn Help, Invitation limit reached](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555)
- LinkedIn Help lists these restriction levels. After a first restriction, wait a few hours. After multiple restrictions in a day, wait a few days. If restricted for **too many outstanding (unaccepted) invitations**, wait **up to one month**. LinkedIn "may suspend or restrict your account" if it suspects an automation tool, and advises adding a personalised message explaining why you want to connect. — [LinkedIn Help, Types of restrictions for sending invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/types-of-restrictions-for-sending-invitations)
- **Unofficial** figures from third parties, mostly LinkedIn-automation vendors with an interest in pushing volume: about **100 invitations a week** for most accounts on a rolling reset, with some advising staying under 80. Premium or high-SSI accounts reportedly get up to 200. That works out to about 15–20 a day. LinkedIn "doesn't disclose one fixed daily or weekly allowance", and unused invitations don't roll over. — [Valley, LinkedIn connection limits 2026](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/linkedin-invitation-limit-in-2025-weekly-limits-more); [Expandi, LinkedIn weekly invitation limit](https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-connections-limit/)
- Sales Navigator Core costs $119.99/month billed monthly (see Q1). — [LinkedIn Sales Navigator plans](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/compare-plans)

**Cold email: Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft sender rules**
- Gmail: since **February 2024**, senders of **5,000+ messages a day to Gmail accounts** must authenticate their mail, avoid unwanted mail, and make unsubscribing easy. Bulk senders should keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools **below 0.10% and never reach 0.30%**. Since **November 2025**, Gmail has been "ramping up its enforcement" on non-compliant mail, including **temporary and permanent rejections**. *(Google primary sources, via search summary.)* — [Google, Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en); [Google, Email sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414?hl=en)
- Yahoo applies the same **0.3% spam-complaint threshold** as Google. Bulk mail must include a **one-click unsubscribe**, and unsubscribes must be processed **within 48 hours**. *(Third-party summaries; I couldn't read Yahoo's primary page.)* — [Clearout, Google and Yahoo bulk sender guidelines](https://clearout.io/blog/google-yahoo-bulk-email-sender-guidelines/); [Mailgun, Yahoogle bulk senders](https://www.mailgun.com/state-of-email-deliverability/chapter/yahoogle-bulk-senders/)
- Microsoft (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live): from **5 May 2025**, senders of **5,000+ emails a day** must pass SPF and DKIM and publish DMARC (at least p=none, aligned with SPF or DKIM). Non-compliant mail is **rejected** with "550 5.7.15 Access denied". — [Microsoft Tech Community announcement](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%e2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%e2%80%90volume-senders/4399730); [DMARC Report, Microsoft May 2025](https://dmarcreport.com/blog/microsoft-outlook-dmarc-requirements-may-2025/)

**Cold email: setup and cost**
- Warm-up advice from cold-email and deliverability vendors varies. One says start at **15–20 emails a day and ramp over 3–4 weeks**, another says **4–8 weeks**. A typical ramp is 5–15 a day in week 1, 15–30 in week 2 and 30–50 in week 3. Live campaigns then run about **25–30 emails per inbox per day**. For **under 50 emails a day, 2 secondary domains and 2–3 mailboxes are enough**. The recommended domain endings are .com, .io and .co. *(All vendor-published. The durations conflict. The search summary mixed the sources.)* — [MailReach, Warm up email domain](https://www.mailreach.co/blog/how-to-warm-up-email-domain); [NameSilo, Warm up a new domain](https://www.namesilo.com/blog/en/email/how-to-warm-up-a-new-domain-for-cold-email-outreach); [EmailBison, Secondary domains](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-secondary-domains); [Smartlead, Secondary domains](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/secondary-domains)
- **Google Workspace Business Starter** costs **$8.40/user/month** on the flexible plan after the 2025 price rise that bundled Gemini. The annual price is reported as **$7/user/month** by one source and **$8** by another (conflict; check the official page). — [Mailbird, Workspace pricing changes 2025](https://www.getmailbird.com/google-workspace-pricing-small-business-guide/); [Name.com, Workspace pricing 2026](https://www.name.com/blog/google-workspace-pricing); [Google Workspace pricing (official)](https://workspace.google.com/pricing)
- **Instantly Growth** costs **$47/month** billed monthly ($37.60 billed annually) for 5,000 emails a month and 1,000 uploaded contacts. **Smartlead Basic** costs **$39/month** ($32.50 annually) for 2,000 leads and 6,000 emails. *(Third-party pricing roundups.)* — [Cleanlist, Instantly pricing 2026](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-09-01-instantly-pricing-guide); [GigRadar, Smartlead vs Instantly](https://gigradar.io/blog/smartlead-vs-instantly)

**Cold email: legality in the UK**
- ICO (PECR): you **can email corporate subscribers (limited companies, LLPs, Scottish partnerships, some government bodies) without consent**, but should keep a "do not email" list and check new lists against it. **Sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals**, so they need specific consent or the soft opt-in. — [ICO, Business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/); [ICO, Electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/)

### Inferences
- **Budget sketch (inference; domain prices not verified):** 2 secondary domains (roughly $10–$15 each a year, unverified), 2 Workspace mailboxes (about $17/month at the flexible rate), and Instantly Growth ($47) or Smartlead Basic ($39) add up to roughly **$70–$95 in month one**. That uses half or more of a $100–$200 budget. Data costs such as TheirStack ($49+) come on top. **The cheapest start is to send a small number of hand-personalised emails straight from Workspace (no sending tool) while the domains warm up, and use LinkedIn in parallel.**
- **Timing:** because warm-up takes 3–8 weeks, cold email **can't produce "fast first revenue" in weeks 1–3**. LinkedIn (free, starts immediately, about 100 invitations a week) is the fastest channel. Cold email adds reach from about week 4.
- **LinkedIn capacity:** about 400 personalised invitations a month. Agency owners and recruiters are heavy LinkedIn users, so a low-volume, message-first approach suits this audience. Avoid automation tools because LinkedIn explicitly restricts suspected automation, and losing the account would cost the founder their main free channel.
- **Rules:** a solo founder sending 20–50 emails a day is nowhere near the 5,000-a-day threshold. Still, set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, include an opt-out line, and keep complaint rates very low, because Gmail enforcement tightened in November 2025. For UK prospects, check whether the agency is a limited company (usually yes) or a sole trader before emailing.

- **Prospect-list directories.** I couldn't research REC member directory, Agency Central, APSCo, American Staffing Association member search, Clutch staffing listings, or Google Maps scraping tools and their prices.
- **Partnerships with recruitment trainers and coaches** (for example affiliate or referral deals): no evidence gathered.
- I didn't verify **Google Workspace's official annual price** (sources conflict) or **domain registration costs**.
- I didn't get **Yahoo's primary sender-requirements page** or the exact all-sender (non-bulk) requirements on Google's page.

## Q3. The "free sample / spec work" approach, B2B cold outreach reply rates (2024–2026), and pilot-to-paid conversion

### Takeaway
Vendor benchmarks put **average cold email reply rates at about 3–5%** (Instantly's platform-wide average is **3.43%**), with top performers at 10–25% on tightly targeted segments. **More than half of replies come from the first email.** I found no hard data on how well free samples convert, or on pilot-to-paid rates for productised services. Treat any specific conversion rate as an assumption to test.

### Cited Findings
- **Instantly Benchmark Report (vendor-published by a cold-email tool, so possibly biased):** platform-wide average reply rate **3.43% for 2025–2026**, with top performers above 10%. **58% of replies come on step 1**, and steps 2–4 bring the other 42%. The best campaigns are **under 80 words**. — [Instantly, Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026); [Instantly, Reply rate benchmarks](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/)
- Average B2B cold email reply rates were reported at **3%–5.1% across 2024–2025**, with top-quartile senders at **15–25%** thanks to better hooks, targeting and follow-ups. *(Growth-agency blog; the search summary didn't name the exact source page.)* — [The Digital Bloom, Cold outbound reply-rate benchmarks 2025](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)
- Reply rates reportedly **fell from 8.5% in 2019 to about 5% in 2025** because of crowded inboxes and stricter spam filters. For B2B, "5–10% is solid, 10–15% excellent, 15%+ best in class on tight segments". *(Cold-email tool and agency blogs, so vendor-biased. The search summary didn't say which page gave which number.)* — [Mailforge, Average cold email response rates 2026](https://www.mailforge.ai/blog/average-cold-email-response-rates); [Reachoutly, Cold email response rate](https://reachoutly.com/cold-email/response-rate/)
- Belkins, a lead-gen agency, publishes a "2026 study" of B2B cold email response rates, but I couldn't get its figures. — [Belkins, B2B cold email response rates](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
- Vendor guides for recruiters say outreach tied to "real hiring triggers (new job postings, funding announcements, leadership changes) generates replies". That supports opening with a specific hiring signal as the hook. *(Vendor content, mixed sources.)* — [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/get-client-staffing-agencies/); [ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/recruiting/staffing-agency-find-clients)
- Comparable products lower the barrier to starting: Agency Leads charges **$95 for the first month instead of $295** (discounted paid pilot, no commitment), and TheirStack offers a **free tier** (50 company credits a month). — [Agency Leads pricing](https://agency-leads.com/en/pricing/); [TheirStack pricing](https://theirstack.com/en/pricing)

### Inferences
- **Funnel maths (planning assumptions, not measurements):** at the ~3.4% vendor average, 500 cold emails give about 17 replies. Only some of those will be positive, and I have no cited figure for how many. For a solo founder, **the realistic first-month target is a few pilot sign-ups, not dozens.** A hyper-specific opener (for example "3 fintechs in Manchester posted 7 backend roles this week; here's who's hiring for them") is the kind of targeted message that vendor data links to above-average reply rates.
- **The free sample is the pitch itself.** The product is information, so sending 3–5 real signals for the prospect's niche in the first message shows the value immediately and costs almost nothing. The risk is that the prospect uses the free leads and never pays. Mitigate by making the full weekly list (more companies, contacts and context) the paid item, and moving to a **paid pilot** (like Agency Leads' discounted first month) instead of a free month.
- Because 58% of replies come from the first email (Instantly), put the sample in email 1 and use 2–3 short follow-ups rather than long sequences.

### Gaps
- I found **no data on how well "free sample / spec work" converts** for data or lead-gen services (searches for Indie Hackers, Reddit and case studies weren't possible after the search budget ran out).
- I found **no benchmark for free-trial or pilot-to-paid conversion** for B2B productised services or small SaaS (for example opt-in vs opt-out trial rates). Needs a follow-up.
- I didn't get **positive-reply rates or meeting-booked rates** from any 2024–2026 benchmark, or any **recruitment/staffing-specific** reply-rate breakdown.
- I found no reliable **LinkedIn DM or connection-acceptance rate benchmarks** (non-vendor).

## Q4. Productised-service best practices: pilot pricing, upfront payment, monthly subscriptions, churn, and low-fee payment tools

### Takeaway
The best evidence I have is how competitors price. They use a **discounted first month that then renews automatically at full price** (Agency Leads: $95 then $295/month, cancel any time), **minimum terms in the UK** (Agency Leads UK: 6-month subscription), and **credits that roll over** (TheirStack). I couldn't gather churn benchmarks, payment-processor fees (Stripe, PayPal, Wise) or rules for student account holders.

### Cited Findings
- Agency Leads (US): **$95 first month, then $295/month renewing automatically, no commitment, cancel any time.** — [Agency Leads pricing](https://agency-leads.com/en/pricing/)
- Agency Leads (UK): **£219/month on a 6-month subscription.** — [Agency Leads UK pricing](https://agency-leads.com/en/pricing/uk/)
- TheirStack: monthly credit plans or a **one-time credit purchase ($109)**; paid credits **roll over for up to 12 months**. — [TheirStack pricing](https://theirstack.com/en/pricing)
- LeadsBrew: a **single fixed-price $99 subscription** sold through Gumroad, which shows a one-tier, checkout-link model is workable for an indie seller. — [Gumroad listing](https://1000what.gumroad.com/l/recruitingbrew)
- Apollo and Sales Navigator both give an annual discount of about 20–25% off monthly prices, a pattern agencies are used to (Sales Nav Core $119.99 monthly vs $89.99/month annual; Apollo Basic about $59 monthly vs $49 annual). — [LinkedIn Sales Navigator plans](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/compare-plans); [Warmly, Apollo pricing](https://www.warmly.ai/p/blog/apollo-pricing)

### Inferences
- **Pilot structure** (copying the competitor pattern): a paid **4-week pilot at about $49–$99, paid upfront**, which then becomes monthly at $99–$199 unless cancelled. Offer a small discount for paying 3 months upfront. This keeps the founder's cash flow positive from week 1 and screens out prospects who aren't serious.
- **One tier, one niche per client** at the start, sold through a checkout link (the LeadsBrew/Gumroad pattern). Add tiers (for example more companies per week, or two niches) only after 3–5 paying clients.
- **Churn risk:** agencies may cancel once they have "enough" leads or when hiring in their niche slows. Assume meaningful monthly churn (no cited figure) and keep adding new clients.

### Gaps
- **Payment tools:** I couldn't verify Stripe, PayPal or Wise fees for US/UK cards, invoicing and currency conversion (stripe.com blocked, search budget exhausted). I also couldn't check whether a student (18+) can open Stripe as an individual or sole proprietor, or the tax/ID requirements. **Get these from the providers' official pricing pages before choosing.**
- **Churn benchmarks** for small B2B data subscriptions or productised services: none gathered.
- **Productised-service playbooks** (for example Indie Hackers or Starter Story guidance on upfront payment, scope creep, delivery SLAs): none gathered.

## Q5. Case studies and founder stories: people selling lead lists, data-as-a-service or BD services to recruiters

### Takeaway
Existing products show the business model works: Agency Leads (discounted first month, then $295/month), JobGrabber (about $99–$299/month) and an indie Gumroad product (LeadsBrew, $99 subscription). But **I couldn't collect any first-person founder stories** (Indie Hackers, Reddit, YouTube, Starter Story) on what worked for getting the first clients.

### Cited Findings
- **Agency Leads** markets itself on hiring intelligence ("you need more than basic firmographics; you need hiring intelligence") and on job counts per company ("knowing exactly the number of job openings… such staffing leads have high chances of conversion"). It publishes a "Staffing Lead ROI Calculator" and SEO comparison pages (for example "Agency Leads vs JobGrabber") as its acquisition content. *(Vendor.)* — [Agency Leads](https://agency-leads.com/); [Agency Leads ROI calculator](https://agency-leads.com/recruitment-sales/staffing-lead-roi-calculator/); [Agency Leads vs JobGrabber](https://agency-leads.com/recruitment-tools/agency-leads-vs-jobgrabber/)
- **JobGrabber** has been selling job-posting-derived leads with hiring-manager contacts to staffing agencies in the US, UK, Canada and Mexico. — [eGrabber JobGrabber](https://www.egrabber.com/jobgrabber/)
- **LeadsBrew/RecruitingBrew** is a $99 fixed-price subscription for small and growing agencies, sold on Gumroad. It's evidence that an indie-scale version of this product exists. I couldn't verify its traction. — [Gumroad listing](https://1000what.gumroad.com/l/recruitingbrew)

### Inferences
- Established players compete on **volume and self-serve software**. The gap a solo founder can fill is **done-for-you, niche-specific curation with context** ("why now", who to contact, suggested angle), delivered weekly. Their acquisition content (ROI calculators, "vs" pages) also suggests agency owners respond to ROI framing, such as "one placement pays for X years".
- The existence of several comparable products also means prospects may say "we already use Agency Leads / JobGrabber". The founder should prepare a one-line answer about curation, niche focus and time saved.

### Gaps
- **No founder stories collected**, for example "I sell leads to recruiters" posts on r/recruiting, r/Entrepreneur or Indie Hackers, YouTube case studies of BD-for-recruiters agencies, or Starter Story interviews. The search budget ran out and reddit.com couldn't be fetched. This is the most important gap for "what worked" evidence.
- Other comparables weren't examined: leadsforrecruiters.com pricing (blocked), UK services such as BD agencies for recruiters, and hiring-signal newsletters.
