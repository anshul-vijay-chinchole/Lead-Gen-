# Hiring-Market Conditions and Niche Selection for a Weekly "Hiring Signal Report" (US/UK, 2024-2026)

Research date: 2026-09-25. Method note: direct page fetches of hiringlab.indeed.com, kpmg.com, bls.gov and staffingindustry.com were blocked by the network proxy in this environment, so figures below come from search-engine extracts of those primary pages (URL cited is the primary page wherever the extract came from it) plus secondary reporting. Treat single-sourced numbers as "verify before publishing". Vendor-published statistics (Bullhorn, Pin, JobsPikr, Robert Half surveys, ResumeUp.AI) are flagged as such.

## 1. Job-posting volume trends 2024-2026 (US and UK): rising or falling, and by sector?

### Takeaway
The US is at an early turning point: Indeed's US Job Postings Index turned positive year-over-year in September 2026 for the first time in almost four years, and JOLTS openings ticked up to 7.27M in July 2026, but gains are concentrated in healthcare, engineering, manufacturing and personal care while tech and white-collar professional services remain well below pre-pandemic levels. The UK is much weaker: ONS vacancies are at a five-year low (702k, Jun-Aug 2026) and Indeed UK postings are about 31% below pre-pandemic, though the KPMG/REC permanent-placements index crossed 50 in August 2026 for the first time since September 2022.

- January 2026 data: candidate availability rose for the 40th consecutive month (redundancies, reduced hiring); Nursing/Medical/Care had the sharpest drop in permanent vacancies and Engineering the softest. — [KPMG/REC, Feb 2026 release](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/02/kpmg-rec-uk-report-on-jobs.html)

### Inferences
- US demand has probably bottomed and is starting to recover (JPI YoY positive, openings up, SIA upgrade, see Q2). The UK is 6-12 months behind: agency placements have only just stabilised while overall vacancies are still falling.
- In both countries the recovery is narrow and sector-specific (engineering, manufacturing, healthcare, AI-adjacent tech, accounting/finance in the UK). A weekly report that filters to the growing pockets fits these conditions better than a broad "who's hiring" feed.
- "Low-hire, low-fire" (quits 1.9%, hires falling) means fewer roles churn naturally, so agencies compete over a smaller pool of open roles. Early knowledge of a new requisition is worth more.

## 2. Recruitment/staffing agency performance 2024-2026: under pressure and hungry for clients, or cutting spend?

### Takeaway
Agencies have had a three-to-four-year downturn, deepest in permanent (fee-based) recruitment. Large listed firms have cut costs heavily (Hays £50M, PageGroup about £40M) while fees are still falling, especially in the UK. 2026 shows the first signs of recovery: SIA raised its US forecast to +2.4%, and Robert Half and KPMG/REC perm indicators have turned up. Agencies are therefore both hungry for new clients and cost-disciplined, and hiring freezes are their stated biggest obstacle to winning new business.

### Inferences
- Permanent/contingent-fee recruitment, the model most small specialist agencies run and the buyers of this product, was hit hardest (Hays perm -12%, Randstad perm -5%). Agencies are client-constrained: their problem is finding companies that are actually hiring, not finding candidates. That is the pain this product addresses.
- Cost-cutting is industry-wide (Hays, PageGroup), so discretionary tools face scrutiny. The product has to be priced and pitched as directly revenue-generating (one extra placement at a 15-25% fee pays for years of subscription), not as "nice-to-have data". Note: typical fee percentages are not sourced in this research.
- The UK has a very long tail of micro agencies (about 79.5% under 10 staff). That makes it a big market of small buyers, but one with elevated insolvency risk, so churn will be structurally high.
- Bullhorn's finding that 56% grew revenue in 2025 conflicts somewhat with listed-firm declines. The likely explanation is survey selection bias (Bullhorn customers, AI adopters) plus small-firm heterogeneity. Treat it as optimistic.

## 3. Which niches have persistent, hard-to-fill demand that agencies place, and which have the most specialised small agencies?

### Takeaway
The most persistent, data-backed demand in 2026 is in: (a) engineering and advanced manufacturing (US JOLTS manufacturing openings at a 2.5-year high, Indeed "strong demand for engineers", UK perm engineering vacancies rising); (b) accounting and finance (UK perm vacancies rising, SIA +2%); (c) construction and skilled trades tied to data centres, energy and fabs (ABC: 349k workers needed in 2026, 456k in 2027); and (d) healthcare (strongest US job growth, but nursing is cooling and dominated by large firms and VMS channels). Tech overall is depressed, but AI/ML and cybersecurity niches are growing. Life sciences is SIA's fastest-growing staffing segment (+5%).

### Inferences
- Best fit for a job-posting-driven signal product: niches where (1) demand is rising now, (2) roles are hard to fill so employers will pay agency fees, (3) buyers are many small specialist agencies, and (4) hiring companies are mid-sized firms that post publicly. That points to engineering/manufacturing, accounting/finance, and construction/trades (especially data-centre, energy and infrastructure contractors).
- Healthcare has strong demand but is a weaker fit. Hospital systems post "evergreen" requisitions continuously, so a new posting says little, and much agency volume flows through MSP/VMS channels controlled by large firms. This is an inference; VMS share was not sourced here.
- Generic tech is still in a downturn for perm agencies. AI/ML and cybersecurity are growing sub-niches but are also the most exposed to AI-driven direct sourcing and in-house TA teams.

## 4. Impact of AI on agency recruitment and on BD tooling

### Takeaway
AI is doing two things at once. It lets employers do more screening and sourcing in-house (a structural threat to contingent agencies, reported in 2026), while agencies adopting AI report faster placements and better growth. For agency business development specifically, AI makes generic outbound cheaper and more saturated, which raises the value of timely, specific, verified hiring signals over raw contact lists.

### Cited Findings
- Bloomberg/Detroit News (18 Feb 2026): AI lets employers screen resumes, rank applicants and run preliminary interviews without external recruiters such as Robert Half, ManpowerGroup and Randstad; combined with a "low-fire, low-hire" market, this squeezes an industry dependent on hiring volumes and placement fees. — [Detroit News (Bloomberg)](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/2026/02/18/ai-threatens-staffing-industry-as-companies-bring-recruitment-in-house/88738387007/)
- LinkedIn Future of Recruiting 2025: 74% of TA professionals say AI makes hiring more efficient; about 37% are experimenting with or integrating gen AI; gen-AI users save roughly a workday per week (about 20% of workload); Recruiter AI pilot users saved 4+ hours per role and reviewed 62% fewer profiles; only 25% feel confident measuring quality of hire. — [LinkedIn Future of Recruiting 2025 (PDF)](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/talent-solutions/resources/pdfs/future-of-recruiting-2025.pdf); [LinkedIn blog](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/future-of-recruiting-2025)
- LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant (announced Oct 2024) became globally available in English in September 2025 (vendor blog source). It automates intake, sourcing, pre-screening and outreach drafting for in-house TA as well as agencies. — [Pin.com blog](https://www.pin.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-new-features/)
- Bullhorn GRID 2026 (vendor survey): agencies using AI at any stage are 3.5x-4.5x more likely to have increased revenue; top-performing firms are 4x more likely to use AI. — [Bullhorn press release](https://www.bullhorn.com/news-and-press/press-releases/bullhorn-grid-report-staffing-firms-using-ai-see-stronger-growth-faster-placements/); [Bullhorn GRID 2026 report](https://www.bullhorn.com/grid/2026-industry-trends/report/)
- Bullhorn GRID Talent Trends 2026 (23 Sep 2026): 92% of candidates interviewed by an AI voice agent rated it as good as or better than a live recruiter interview. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/bullhorns-2026-grid-talent-trends-143000510.html)
- SIA flags AI as a potential disruptor both of client demand for IT workers and of how staffing is delivered; about 85% of staffing firms increased AI use in 2025 (secondary summary). — [SIA IT staffing report](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/it-staffing-growth-could-return-though-ai-may-rewrite-the-rules)
- Robert Half (a staffing firm, so self-interested) survey: 67% of hiring managers say they are more likely to seek staffing-firm support to navigate AI-related hiring challenges. — [Robert Half, AI in Recruiting](https://www.roberthalf.com/us/en/insights/ai-hiring-impact)
- AI is reshaping demand mix: LinkUp shows entry-level listings about 70% below the 2022 peak and customer-service listings down about 30%, while AI-implementation roles surge. Indeed UK AI-mention postings were +127% vs baseline (Feb 2026). — [LinkUp](https://www.linkup.com/insights/blog/inside-the-ai-revolution-linkup-job-data-shows-steady-hiring); [Indeed Hiring Lab UK](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/uk/blog/2026/03/30/uk-labour-market-is-cooling-but-ai-hiring-is-bucking-the-trend/)
- Indeed Hiring Lab (Jul 2026) published analyses titled "AI and Job Postings: From Destruction to Creation?" and "The Labor Market Is Tilting Toward Seniority"; details could not be extracted. — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/); [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/23/the-labor-market-is-tilting-toward-seniority/)

### Inferences
- Where AI makes in-house hiring easy (high-volume, entry-level, generic white-collar and tech roles), clients are likelier to cut agencies out. Agencies in hard-to-fill, niche, senior or licensed-trade roles keep more pricing power. That favours engineering, trades and specialist finance for the product's buyers.
- Since agencies are adopting AI for sourcing and outreach, the scarce input moves upstream to knowing which companies have a live, unmet need this week and who owns the budget. That is the product's thesis. The flip side: Bullhorn, LinkedIn and ATS/CRM vendors can bundle similar "job-change" or "hiring-signal" features, a platform-competition risk (not sourced; inferred from vendor AI roadmaps).
- The seniority tilt (fewer entry-level roles) suggests reports should prioritise mid/senior, specialist roles, which are also where agency fees are highest (inference; fee data not sourced here).

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) 2025-2026 data quantifying agency fee-rate compression or share of hires shifting from agencies to in-house.
- LinkedIn has not been confirmed to have published a "Future of Recruiting 2026" edition; only 2025 was found.
- No sourced data on agencies' BD tool spend (e.g., ZoomInfo/Apollo usage by recruiters).

## 5. Signals beyond job postings that correlate with hiring need (funding, expansions, reposted/long-open roles): evidence

### Takeaway
Evidence for funding rounds as a hiring predictor has weakened sharply: post-Series A hiring rates in 2024-2025 are lower than in 2020-2022, and top-quartile raisers did not grow headcount faster. Raw job-posting counts are noisy because 18-27% of postings may be "ghost jobs". Long-open or reposted roles are ambiguous: they mark either hard-to-fill roles (the best agency opportunities) or ghost listings, so they need filtering. Time-to-fill rising (LinkUp 47.7 days) supports "long-open role" as a useful agency signal if validated.

### Cited Findings
- Revelio Labs: median Series A startup headcount fell from 57 (2020) to 44 (2024); Series A capital per employee rose from about $160k (2020) to more than $320k (2025); post-Series A hiring rates in 2024 and 2025 are several percentage points lower than in 2020-2022, attributed to AI and product-led growth allowing revenue scaling without headcount. — [Revelio Labs](https://www.reveliolabs.com/news/tech/startups-are-hiring-less-and-raising-more)
- Tomasz Tunguz: startups raising top-quartile Seed-through-Series C rounds grew headcount about 6% over the last twelve months vs about 12% for other companies (difference not statistically significant), so bigger raises did not mean faster hiring. — [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/headcount_growth_and_capital_raised/)
- Greenhouse (2025): 18-22% of online job postings are ghost jobs; ResumeUp.AI (2025): 27.4% of US LinkedIn listings likely ghost jobs, using a >30-days-old heuristic (vendor analyses; methodology debatable). — [CNBC, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/11/ghost-job-postings-add-another-layer-of-uncertainty-to-stalled-jobs-picture.html); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/one-quarter-of-jobs-posted-online-are-fake-ghost-jobs-study/496683)
- LiveCareer survey (Mar 2025, 918 HR professionals): 45% say they "regularly" post ghost jobs and 48% "occasionally". — [Clarify Capital summary](https://clarifycapital.com/ghost-jobs); [Wikipedia: Ghost job](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_job)
- LinkUp: average closed-job duration rose 6.2% to 47.73 days in Q1 2026, indicating greater hiring friction and more selective employers. — [LinkUp Q1 2026](https://www.linkup.com/insights/blog/q1-2026-economic-indicator-report)
- Vendor data (JobsPikr): skilled-trade postings (electrician/HVAC) stay open about twice as long as tech roles. — [JobsPikr](https://www.jobspikr.com/blog/talent-scarcity-2026-hiring-shortages/)
- Megaproject-driven demand (AI data centres, semiconductor fabs) is named by ABC as a key driver of construction labour needs, which supports capex/facility-expansion announcements as a leading signal for trades and engineering hiring. — [ABC via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/15/3219391/0/en/abc-construction-industry-must-attract-349-000-workers-in-2026-despite-macroeconomic-headwinds.html)

### Inferences
- For this product, the strongest composite signal is probably: a new or recently reposted role in a hard-to-fill niche, plus the same employer with 3+ open roles or an open duration beyond the niche median, plus a supporting event (facility expansion, contract win, leadership hire). Funding should be a secondary, weak signal, and mainly relevant to tech and life sciences.
- Ghost-job prevalence is a product-quality risk. Deduplication, reposting detection, and checks that the careers page also shows the role are necessary to keep agency trust.
- "Long-open role" is itself the agency pitch ("you've had this open 60 days; we specialise in it"). Surfacing days-open is likely a differentiating field.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or independent study was found quantifying how well facility-expansion announcements, contract awards or leadership changes predict job postings or hires.
- No evidence on the conversion rate of agency outreach triggered by hiring signals vs cold outreach.

## 6. Implications: best 2-3 niches and regions to start with, and timing risks

### Takeaway
Timing is favourable: late 2026 sits at the early-recovery inflection. Agencies are still client-starved after 3-4 years of decline, but US demand is turning up and UK perm placements have just crossed 50, so agency budgets for revenue-generating tools should improve into 2027. The evidence best supports launching in the US with (1) engineering and advanced manufacturing and (2) construction and skilled trades tied to data centres, energy and infrastructure, and adding (3) accounting and finance, which is the best UK-led niche. Healthcare and generic tech should be deferred.

### Cited Findings (evidence underpinning the implications)
- US turning point: JPI YoY positive for the first time in about four years (Sep 2026); SIA's 2026 forecast raised to +2.4% with 9 of 11 segments growing; Robert Half perm +3% (Q2 2026); ASA temp/contract +5.8% YoY (Sep 2026). — [Indeed Sep 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/09/24/us-labor-market-snapshot-september-2026/); [SIA Sep 2026](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/us-staffing-industry-forecast-september-2026-update); [Robert Half Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000315213/000031521326000040/rhiq22026ex991.htm); [ASA](https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/09/22/staffing-index-edges-up-in-september/)
- UK lagging but stabilising: perm placements index 50.5 in Aug 2026 (first rise since Sep 2022), yet overall staff demand has fallen for 34 months and ONS vacancies are at a 5-year low; PageGroup UK Q2 gross profit -5.3%. — [KPMG/REC Sep 2026](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/09/kpmg-and-rec-uk-report-on-jobs-september-2026.html); [ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/bulletins/jobsandvacanciesintheuk/latest); [DirectorsTalk](https://www.directorstalkinterviews.com/pagegroup-q2-gross-profit-slips-0-2-as-americas-and-asia-grow/4121256043)
- Buyer pain matches the product: "hiring freezes and budget constraints" are the biggest obstacles to winning new business, and attracting new clients is a top-4 SMB priority. — [Bullhorn GRID 2026](https://www.bullhorn.com/grid/2026-industry-trends/report/)
- Geopolitical and policy shocks have caused sharp swings in 2026 (Iran war and UK political turbulence gave the quickest perm fall in 10 months in May 2026); US immigration enforcement is tightening trade labour supply. — [KPMG/REC May 2026](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/05/kpmg-and-rec-uk-report-on-jobs.html); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/23/america-construction-shortage-trump-immigration-crackdown-ice-deportations-home-costs/)
- Structural risk: AI enabling in-house recruitment (Feb 2026) and agency insolvency rising in the UK through 2023/24. — [Detroit News (Bloomberg)](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/2026/02/18/ai-threatens-staffing-industry-as-companies-bring-recruitment-in-house/88738387007/); [Recruiter](https://www.recruiter.co.uk/news/2024/04/rise-insolvencies-recruitment-agencies)

### Inferences
**Recommended starting niches (ranked)**
1. **US engineering and advanced manufacturing** (mechanical/electrical/controls/process engineers, plant leadership). Rising openings (JOLTS manufacturing at a 2.5-year high), Indeed "strong demand for engineers", SIA engineering +3%, and hard-to-fill roles posted by mid-sized firms whose postings are real signals. Risk: tariff- and auto-cycle-sensitive (Hays engineering -15% on German auto).
2. **US construction and skilled trades / data-centre and energy infrastructure** (project managers, estimators, electricians, MEP engineers, superintendents). ABC's 349k (2026) rising to 456k (2027) requirement, megaproject-driven, long-open trade roles, immigration-tightened supply. Many small regional trade and construction agencies. Risk: interest-rate and construction-spending sensitivity; many trade hires are made via word-of-mouth rather than postings.
3. **Accounting and finance (UK first, then US)**. The only UK category besides engineering with rising perm vacancies (Aug 2026), SIA F&A +2%, Robert Half perm recovering. Very large base of small specialist agencies (inference). Risk: most exposed of the three to AI automation of junior finance roles; target qualified/mid-senior roles.

**Deprioritise for launch**: healthcare/nursing (demand real but evergreen postings, VMS/MSP gatekeeping, UK nursing postings weak, SIA +1%); generic tech/software (postings about 27% below pre-pandemic, Hays tech -11%, highest AI-disintermediation exposure), though AI/ML and cyber could be a later premium add-on (APSCo AI vacancies +41.8%). Life sciences (SIA +5%) is a credible 4th niche but was not deeply researched here.

**Region**: Start US-first (larger agency base of about 27k firms and 54k offices per ASA, demand already recovering). Add UK second, focused on engineering and accounting/finance, where KPMG/REC shows rising perm vacancies. The UK's micro-agency base is large (79.5% micro) but price-sensitive and under insolvency pressure.

**Timing risks**
- *Recovery stalls*: the 2026 upturn is thin (JPI +0.7% YoY; UK perm index barely above 50) and was interrupted once already (May 2026 shock). A relapse would shrink both signals and agency budgets.
- *Counter-cyclical value*: when markets heat up (possibly 2027), agencies become candidate-constrained rather than client-constrained, so demand for client-lead products may soften. Positioning around "hard-to-fill, long-open roles plus decision-makers" hedges this, because those stay valuable in hot markets.
- *AI disintermediation* of agencies in high-volume and junior roles, and bundling of hiring-signal features by incumbents (LinkedIn, Bullhorn, data vendors).
- *Data-quality risk*: 18-27% ghost-job prevalence could erode trust if signals aren't validated.
- *Buyer churn*: small-agency insolvency and cost-cutting mean high logo churn. Pricing should be low enough, or tied to placements, to survive budget reviews.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence on small agencies' willingness to pay for lead/signal data, or on the size of existing competitors in "hiring signal" data for recruiters (likely covered by other researchers).
- No sourced agency counts by niche to size each niche's buyer pool.
- Life sciences and legal niches were not researched in enough depth to rank confidently.
