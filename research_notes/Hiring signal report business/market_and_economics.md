## Q1. How big is the staffing/recruitment industry (US, UK, global; 2024-2026)?

### Takeaway
The global staffing market was about US$620bn in 2024 (SIA). The US is the largest single market: about $184bn in 2024, falling to about $179bn in 2025 after three years of decline, with SIA forecasting about 2-2.4% growth to about $183bn in 2026. The UK is the third-largest market, at about US$50.8bn (SIA, 2024). The REC puts UK recruitment industry turnover at about £42.9bn, or £40.6bn of GVA (2024/25). Most of this revenue is temp/contract staffing. Permanent placement, the fee-based business a "Hiring Signal Report" would serve, is a minority of the total.

- In the UK, about 77% of GVA comes from temp/contract. Perm/search, the contingency-fee business most relevant to a "who is hiring" signal, is roughly a quarter of UK sector value. That is still on the order of £10bn of GVA (a rough inference from 23.3% × £40.6bn).
- Timing matters for the pitch. Perm placements in the UK fell by a third in 2024 and US staffing revenue fell three years running (2023-2025). Job-order scarcity is the dominant pain. The market is forecast to recover modestly in 2026 (US +2-2.4%, UK GVA +4.4%).

## Q2. How many recruitment/staffing firms exist in the US and UK, and how many are small? How fragmented is the market?

### Takeaway
The UK has about **31,000 recruitment enterprises** (31,345 in 2025 per REC). About **80% are micro businesses with fewer than 10 employees**, and about 82% have turnover under £1m (2022 data). The US has somewhere between **about 20,000 and about 27,000 staffing/recruiting firms**, depending on the source and definition. The market is highly fragmented at the bottom, even though large firms hold about 40% of global revenue. That long tail of small, specialist agencies is the target customer base.

### Cited Findings
**UK**
- REC RISR 2024/25: **31,345 UK recruitment enterprises in 2025, 31,225 in 2024 and 29,635 in 2023**. The number of enterprises grew even as perm placements fell — [REC RISR 2024/25 (via search summary)](https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/research/recruitment-and-industry-status-report/uk-recruitment-industry-status-report-202425); [Kenect summary](https://www.kenectrecruitment.co.uk/insights/recruitment-industry-status-report-24-25/)
- RISR (2022 data): **30,035 UK recruitment agencies in 2022** (+6.5% year on year) and **201,154 people working in UK recruitment agencies**. **79.5% are micro-businesses with fewer than 10 employees**. **82% of recruitment enterprises had less than £1m turnover** — [New Millennia (citing REC RISR)](https://www.newmillennia.co.uk/blog/12-recruitment-agency-statistics-you-need-to-know/)
- An alternative count based on ONS data gives about **38,000 recruitment agencies in the UK in 2021**. This conflicts with the REC count of about 30k and probably reflects a broader definition — [Gaffney Zoppi](https://www.gaffneyzoppi.com/blog/discover-how-many-recruitment-agencies-are-in-the-uk-today)
- A claim that the number of UK agencies has grown "on average 27% per year since 2008" (attributed to Companies House) appears on secondary blogs. It is **implausible at face value, since it would compound to far more than 30k firms; treat it as unreliable** — [New Millennia](https://www.newmillennia.co.uk/blog/12-recruitment-agency-statistics-you-need-to-know/); [Gaffney Zoppi](https://www.gaffneyzoppi.com/blog/discover-how-many-recruitment-agencies-are-in-the-uk-today)
- Greg Savage has published "10 facts about UK recruitment", covering the number of agencies and the scale of small operators — [The Savage Truth, Mar 2023](https://thesra.co/2023/03/07/10-shocking-facts-about-uk-recruitment/) (specific figures not retrieved)

**US**
- IBISWorld: **19,783 "Employment & Recruiting Agencies" businesses in the US in 2025 (-2.2% vs 2024)**. The same page summary says **22,370 in 2026 (+2.4%)** and an average of +2.2% a year from 2021 to 2026. **These numbers are internally inconsistent**, since 19,783 × 1.024 ≈ 20,258. They are probably different vintages mixed in the search summary — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/employment-recruiting-agencies/1463/)
- ASA: "around **27,000 staffing and recruiting companies** in the U.S., which altogether operate close to **54,000 offices**" (dated 2021 in the search summary) — [ASA Staffing Industry Statistics](https://americanstaffing.net/research/fact-sheets-analysis-staffing-industry-trends/staffing-industry-statistics/)

The 2022 Economic Census firm-size tables were released in April 2025 — [Census CBP 2022](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2022/econ/cbp/2022-cbp.html); [Census Establishment and Firm Size release, 2025](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/establishment-and-firm-size-statistics.html); [Census SUSB 2022](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html)

**Fragmentation / concentration**
- SIA: the **largest global staffing firms represent about 40% of the market** (headline) — [SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/largest-global-staffing-firms-represent-40-of-market)
- The US, Japan and UK together account for just under 49% of global staffing revenue — [SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/forecast-estimates-us-staffing-industrys-cumulative-growth-by-2030)
- US: the largest IT staffing firms generate **$27.2bn** and the largest healthcare staffing firms **$33bn** in revenue (SIA lists; headline figures) — [SIA IT](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/largest-us-it-staffing-firms-generate-27.2b-in-revenue); [SIA healthcare](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/largest-us-healthcare-staffing-firms-generate-33b-in-revenue)
- ATLAS 2026 benchmark (1,000+ agency recruiters; vendor): **41.1% of respondents said their agency primarily employs full-desk recruiters**, meaning one person runs the whole cycle from client development to placement. This is typical of small agencies — [ATLAS State of Agency Recruitment 2026](https://recruitwithatlas.com/resources/agency-recruitment-report-2026/); [PDF](https://recruitwithatlas.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Atlas-Report-The-State-of-Agency-Recruitment-2026-Benchmark.pdf)

### Inferences
- If about 79.5% of about 31,000 UK enterprises are micro (<10 staff), there are roughly **24,000-25,000 UK micro recruitment businesses**. This applies the 2022 micro share to the 2025 enterprise count, so it is an estimate. If "1-20 employees" is the target, the UK addressable count is probably about 26,000-28,000, but I found no 10-19 band figure.
- In the US, applying a similar small-firm share to about 20,000-27,000 firms suggests **roughly 15,000-22,000 small US agencies**. This is only an estimate: I found no US employee-size distribution.
- The number of UK enterprises rose (29,635 → 31,345) while perm placements fell 33.5%. More agencies competing for fewer vacancies should raise the value of early vacancy intelligence. It also means many new micro agencies face tight budgets.
- Full-desk recruiters (41% per ATLAS) do both BD and delivery. They are the most time-poor buyers and the most likely to value pre-researched leads.

## Q3. Placement-fee economics: fee %, retained fees, average fee values, fill rates, time-to-fill, and the value of one extra job order

### Takeaway
Contingency perm fees cluster at **15-25% of first-year base salary, with about 20% the most-cited benchmark**. In the UK, standard roles are often 15-20% and specialist roles 20-30%. Retained search runs **25-35% of first-year total compensation, typically about a third**, paid in three instalments. One UK placement at £40k-£70k is worth about **£7,000-£12,250** in fees. Contingency fill rates are well below 100%, often cited at 10-55% depending on definition. So the expected value of **one extra relevant job order** for a small agency is plausibly **in the low thousands of £/$**, and one converted placement is worth far more than any plausible subscription price.

### Cited Findings
**Contingency fees**
- Contingency fees are commonly **15-25% of first-year salary with 20% as the benchmark** (SHRM cited as source for 20% most common). Entry-level roles are about 10-15%, mid/senior about 20-25%, and executive or highly specialised roles about 25-30% or more — [Manatal](https://www.manatal.com/blog/recruitment-fees); [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/how-much-do-recruitment-agencies-charge/); [Valuable Recruitment 2026](https://valuablerecruitment.com/blog/recruitment-agency-fees); [Glozo 2026](https://www.glozo.com/blog/recruitment-agency-fees); [Pin 2026](https://www.pin.com/blog/negotiate-recruiter-fees/)
- SHRM 2025 Benchmarking (cited by Pin): average **cost-per-hire $5,475 for non-executive roles and $35,879 for executive roles**. These are total hiring costs, not agency fees — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/negotiate-recruiter-fees/)
- **UK:** standard contingency perm is about **15-20%** of first-year salary. Specialist, senior or hard-to-fill roles are **20-30%+**, and tech roles about **18-22%**. Worked example: a **£40,000 salary at 17.5% = £7,000 fee; £70,000 = £12,250** — [On3works UK 2026](https://on3works.com/resources/recruitment-agency-fees-uk); [Recruitly 2026](https://recruitly.io/resources/recruitment-agency-fees-2026); [Matrix Personnel](https://matrix-personnel.com/recruitment-agency-fees-uk/); [Get Staffed](https://get-staffed.com/how-much-do-recruitment-agencies-charge/); [Chameleoni](https://www.chameleoni.com/pricing-models) (UK agency/vendor guides; the search summary aggregated them, so the per-claim attribution among these URLs is approximate)

**Retained / executive search**
- Retained search fees are typically **25-35% of first-year total compensation** (base plus projected bonus). The **AESC-referenced average is about 30-33%**. Fees are usually paid in **thirds**: on engagement, on shortlist, and on placement or offer acceptance — [KiTalent](https://kitalent.com/executive-search-fees); [The Good Search](https://tgsus.com/executive-search-blog/executive-search-fees-search-firm-pricing/); [Staffing Advisors](https://www.staffingadvisors.com/blog/what-do-executive-search-firms-charge/); [Hunter Recruiting](https://www.hirecruiting.com/newsroom/how-much-do-executive-search-firms-charge/)

**Productivity, fill rates and conversion (vendor datasets)**
- Recruiterflow "Economics of Recruiting" benchmark (**2,100+ search and recruiting firms** on its platform; vendor data):
  - Top-quartile firms generate about **$168K more revenue per recruiter** than the rest.
  - Top-quartile firms make **5.21 placements per recruiter per year vs 1.38 for everyone else**. These are low absolute numbers; the dataset probably includes part-time or low-usage accounts.
  - Screen-to-submission conversion is **50.1% vs 36.1%**.
  - Top-quartile firms produce about 3x the submissions and about 4x the placements per recruiter.
  - A **10% lift in revenue per placement corresponds to a 5.7% lift in revenue per recruiter**.
  - Only about **3% of sourced candidates reach client submission**, **11.3% of screened candidates** are submitted, and the average firm needs **about 7 submissions per hire**.
  - **71% of placements came from candidates already in the CRM** before the job opened.

  Sources: [Recruiterflow benchmarks](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/recruiting-benchmarks/); [Revenue per recruiter benchmark](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/revenue-per-recruiter-benchmark/); [Hunt Scanlon on Recruiterflow data](https://huntscanlon.com/what-the-top-25-percent-of-search-firms-do-differently-and-why-its-not-about-sourcing/)
- Time-to-fill (Recruiterflow): strong perm desks fill mid-level roles in about **28-35 days**; executive searches take **60-90 days** — [Recruiterflow time-to-fill benchmarks](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/time-to-fill-benchmarks/)
- SIA (cited secondarily): temp staffing time-to-fill is normally **4-14 days, median 9 days** — [Ascen](https://www.ascen.com/blog/kpis-for-staffing-agencies); [Conexis](https://www.conexisvmssoftware.com/blog/contingent-workforce-staffing-kpis)
- Contingency fill rates: one aggregator claims agencies working mainly on contingency see **40-55% fill rates**, because several agencies compete for the same role (low-quality source) — [BusinessDojo](https://dojobusiness.com/blogs/news/recruitment-fill-rate). A vendor KPI guide treats a placement-to-job-order ratio **falling from 25% to 10%** as a red flag, which implies healthy ratios nearer 20-25% — [Wiggli](https://www.wiggli.io/blog/6-metrics-recruitment-agencies-should-lookout-for-and-how-to-rescue-them). **These two sources conflict; I found no authoritative industry fill-rate survey.**
- Recruiterflow data: among fast-growing firms, **new-client revenue declines as a share of total revenue** as firms scale, and repeat business becomes the main revenue driver — [Recruiterflow](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/recruitment-business-development/)

**Market-condition context**
- REC member data: **35% of recruitment firms experienced bad debt** in the past year, against 29% across all sectors — [search summary of REC/RISR](https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/research/recruitment-and-industry-status-report/uk-recruitment-industry-status-report-202425)

### Inferences
- **Value of one placement for a small agency (illustrative):**
  - UK: a £40k-£70k role at 17.5% gives a **£7,000-£12,250 fee**, using the cited worked example.
  - US: a mid-level specialist role at an assumed $80k-$120k salary and 20% gives **$16,000-$24,000**. The salary band is my assumption, not a sourced figure.
- **Expected value of one extra relevant job order:** fee × probability of filling.
  - At a 20-25% fill ratio (Wiggli-implied), a £7k-£12k fee gives about **£1,400-£3,000 per job order**.
  - At a 40-55% fill rate (BusinessDojo), the same fee gives about £2,800-£6,700.
  - A US $16k-$24k fee at 25-50% gives about **$4,000-$12,000 per job order**.

  These are derived estimates, not sourced figures.
- A weekly lead report only needs to produce **one extra job order a year that converts** to pay back a subscription of a few hundred £/$ a month many times over. The harder question is conversion from lead to job order, which is covered in Q4/Q5.
- Recruiterflow finds that 71% of placements come from existing CRM candidates and that revenue per placement is the strongest lever. So agencies may value leads for roles in their existing niche, where they already have candidates, much more than generic "company X is hiring" lists. Product design should filter by niche, seniority and salary band.
- With placements per recruiter this low (1.38 a year for non-top-quartile firms in Recruiterflow's data), each additional placement is a material share of a small firm's annual revenue.

### Gaps
- No authoritative (non-vendor) contingency fill-rate statistic. The figures found conflict.
- No retrieved average fee in £ or $ from a primary survey (for example REC, APSCo, or a US fee survey). The per-placement values above are derived from fee percentages.
- No retrieved "average perm fee" from REC/APSCo, or average salary of agency-placed candidates.
- No data on how many job orders a small agency works on at a time, or how many it would need to add.

## Q4. How do agencies win new clients/job orders today, how much time goes to BD, and what are the top challenges?

### Takeaway
Winning new business is the top strategic priority and the top pain in industry surveys. Bullhorn GRID 2025 found **77% of firms focused on growing market share through new-client acquisition**. For the third year running, Bullhorn GRID 2026 reports **falling job volumes plus tight talent pools** as firms' top challenges. **Hiring freezes and budget constraints** are the biggest obstacle to winning new business, and **competition from other agencies** is the second and growing. The main BD methods are phone, LinkedIn, email sequences, referrals and candidate-led (MPC) marketing. Vendor data says outreach tied to a trigger far outperforms cold outreach.

### Cited Findings
**Industry surveys on challenges and priorities**
- **Bullhorn GRID 2026 Industry Trends** (nearly **2,300** recruitment professionals globally; published Feb 25, 2026; vendor):
  - "For a third year, firms identify the paradox of **tight talent pools and falling job volumes** as their top challenges for 2026."
  - "**Hiring freezes and budget constraints** are the biggest obstacles to winning new business."
  - "**Competition from other firms** is now the second biggest obstacle to winning new business and a growing concern."
  - **56% reported revenue growth** (vs 40% the year before), and **13% grew more than 25%**.
  - Firms using AI at any stage were **3.5x-4.5x more likely** to report higher revenue, and top performers are **4x as likely** to use AI.

  Sources: [Bullhorn GRID 2026 report](https://www.bullhorn.com/grid/2026-industry-trends/report/); [Bullhorn press release](https://www.bullhorn.com/news-and-press/press-releases/bullhorn-grid-report-staffing-firms-using-ai-see-stronger-growth-faster-placements/); [GlobeNewswire, 25 Feb 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/25/3244739/0/en/bullhorn-grid-report-staffing-firms-using-ai-see-stronger-growth-faster-placements.html)
- **Bullhorn GRID 2025 Industry Trends** (vendor):
  - Agencies "prioritized attracting new clients over all other concerns", and **77% of firms focused on increasing market share through new client acquisition**.
  - Firms had seen **declining revenue for three years**.
  - **40% saw revenue growth**, and nearly a quarter grew 10% or more.
  - **67%** had invested in or were experimenting with generative AI.

## Q5. Evidence that "company posted a job" is used as a BD trigger (job-board BD, spec CV / MPC, vacancy-led BD), and how effective it is

### Takeaway
Vacancy-led BD is a long-established agency practice: monitoring job boards and calling the companies that posted, often with a spec CV or MPC. Several vendors already productise it (eGrabber JobGrabber, Agency Leads, Signalbase, Recruit Signals), which is direct evidence of demand and of competition. Effectiveness data is thin and comes from vendors. Job-posting intent is claimed to lift response rates about 5x over baseline. Practitioners warn that advertised vacancies attract competing agencies, and that stale postings (open 45+ days) signal pain and are better targets than fresh ones.

### Cited Findings
- **eGrabber JobGrabber** is marketed as "#1 Lead Generation Software for Recruitment Agencies". It "scans job boards and finds companies actively hiring — **with decision-maker contacts included**" and "automates lead generation for staffing agencies". This is essentially the same value proposition as a Hiring Signal Report — [eGrabber Staffing Agency Leads](https://www.egrabber.com/staffing-agency-leads/); [eGrabber JobGrabber](https://www.egrabber.com/jobgrabber/)
- **Agency Leads** advertises "**229K+ verified hiring companies**" for recruiters and publishes guides on using job boards to win clients — [Agency Leads](https://agency-leads.com/en/solutions/agency-leads/); [Agency Leads: Job Boards](https://agency-leads.com/en/recruitment-tools/job-boards/)
- **Signalbase** markets "Lead Generation for Recruiters & Agencies" — [Signalbase](https://www.trysignalbase.com/)
- **Recruit Signals** publishes BD playbooks and a hiring-intent product for agencies. It argues that reactive, vacancy-led BD worked in a high-vacancy market "but that market is gone": vacancy counts are lower, decision cycles longer and hiring managers slower. Competitors win by "identifying hiring intent early, building relationships before roles are posted" — [Recruit Signals: BD playbook for a stabilising market](https://recruitsignals.com/intelligence/articles/recruitment-agency-bd-playbook-stabilising-market)
- Recruiterflow's trigger-based BD playbook names "**a role in your niche that's been posted for 45+ days without moving** (pain point, not just budget)" as a conversion signal. It also describes ATS automations that fire when a job enters or leaves a saved search — [Recruiterflow trigger-based BD](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/trigger-based-recruitment-bd/); [Recruiterflow help: Job Saved Search Triggers](https://help.recruiterflow.com/en/articles/9896848-how-to-use-job-saved-search-triggers-in-recipes)
- "Signal-based selling on **job-posting intent gets 18% response rates vs. the 3.4% industry baseline** (Autobound, 2026)" — vendor-cited, original not verified — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/how-to-get-clients-staffing-agency/)
- Trigger outreach in general (a leadership change) gets about 14% response vs about 1.2% for cold calls — [Recruiterflow](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/trigger-based-recruitment-bd/)
- **MPC (Most Placeable Candidate) marketing** is described as a "well-known strategy". Recruiters contact hiring managers to say they have finished a search for a similar role and have strong candidates. It is often paired with job-board-sourced targets — [search summary citing eGrabber / Agency Leads materials](https://www.egrabber.com/staffing-agency-leads/); [ATLAS candidate-led BD](https://recruitwithatlas.com/blog/recruitment-business-development/)
- Practitioner forum threads on recruitment BD discuss calling on job adverts as a standard starter tactic — [InterviewHelp: "Recruitment BD, Advice please"](https://www.interviewhelp.io/blog/posts/recruitment_bd_advice_please_/)
- JobAdder and Kondo describe the future of recruitment BD as moving from reactive to proactive, signal- and relationship-led approaches — [JobAdder](https://jobadder.com/blog/the-future-of-business-development-in-recruitment-what-comes-next/); [Kondo](https://www.trykondo.com/blog/modern-recruitment-bd-techniques)
- Contrary view: Greg Savage warns against blind cold-calling and mass LinkedIn outreach — [The Savage Truth](https://thesra.co/2023/08/14/17-selling-tips-for-recruiters-now/)

### Inferences
- The existence of several paid products doing "job-board scan + decision-maker contact" (JobGrabber, Agency Leads, Signalbase, Recruit Signals) **validates willingness to pay**. It also means a solo founder's report must **differentiate**. Possible angles:
  - niche or specialism filtering
  - UK/US-specific coverage
  - verified hiring-manager contacts, not generic HR inboxes
  - freshness plus "stale posting" (45+ day) flags
  - evidence that the company uses agencies, or has no in-house TA team
  - a done-for-you weekly format rather than a self-serve tool
- Public job postings are visible to every agency, so the effectiveness of pure "posted a job" leads erodes as competition rises (Bullhorn: competition is the number-two obstacle). Adding pain signals (days open, repeated reposts, multiple roles in the same function) and speed (weekly or faster delivery) is likely to matter more than raw volume.
- The claimed 5x uplift (18% vs 3.4%) and Recruiterflow's 14% vs 1.2% support the principle that signal-led outreach beats cold outreach. Both are vendor numbers, so the report should present them as indicative.

### Gaps
- No independent study measuring conversion from "job posting → BD call → job order → placement" for agencies.
- No pricing data collected for JobGrabber, Agency Leads, Signalbase or Recruit Signals. This is outside this note's scope, but important for competitive positioning.
- No quantified data on what share of employers who post publicly also use agencies for the same role, or how many agencies typically contact a company per posting.
- Could not retrieve UK trainer content (Recruitment Juice, Recruitment Entrepreneur) on vacancy-led BD and spec-CV effectiveness, because the web-search budget was exhausted.
