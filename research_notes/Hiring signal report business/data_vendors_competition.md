# Competitive Landscape: Hiring-Signal / Job-Postings Data Vendors and Sales-Intelligence Platforms (substitutes for a weekly "Hiring Signal Report" sold to small recruitment agencies)

Research date: 2026-09-25. All prices are "as seen" on this date and change often. **Method caveat (applies to every section):** direct page fetches (WebFetch) to every vendor domain and to most third-party blogs were blocked by this environment's egress proxy, so every figure below comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the pages directly. The URL given is the result the claim was attributed to. Where the summary did not make clear which of several results a figure came from, this is flagged. Check key prices on the live pricing pages before publishing. Tags: **[vendor]** = the vendor's own marketing/docs; **[competitor-authored]** = a comparison or pricing blog written by a rival sales-tool vendor, which has an incentive to make the subject look expensive or bad; **[3rd-party]** = directory, review site, or press.

## Q1. Job-postings data vendors: what they sell, to whom, and at what price

### Takeaway
Raw job-postings data is sold at every price level. Developer-friendly self-serve APIs start at $0–$49/month (TheirStack, PredictLeads, Coresignal, Techmap, JobsPikr), with pay-per-record scraping from about $0.10–$2.50 per 1,000 jobs (Apify, Bright Data). At the top end, enterprise or research feeds are sold by quote and run to five and six figures a year (LinkUp, Revelio Labs, Lightcast). Only TheirStack names recruitment agencies as a target and ships a no-code app with email alerts. Every other vendor here is an API or dataset product aimed at developers, data teams, investors, or labour-market analysts. None of them provides decision-maker contacts.

### Inferences
- The "self-serve" layer (TheirStack, Techmap, PredictLeads, Coresignal Mini, JobsPikr Kick-off) makes job data cheap. Getting a raw list of companies hiring for X in region Y costs $0–$79/month. **TheirStack is the most direct substitute.** It is no-code, alerts on job titles and keywords, dedupes across sources, has a free tier, and markets to recruiting agencies. A solo founder should treat it as the benchmark and also as a candidate upstream data source.
- The enterprise vendors (LinkUp, Revelio, Lightcast) are not realistic substitutes for small agencies, because of price and delivery format (warehouse feeds, analytics). They matter only as upstream data options at scale.
- No job-data vendor here supplies decision-maker contacts. Contacts have to come from a sales-intelligence tool (Q2), which leaves a join step (company to hiring manager to verified email) that a report can do for the agency.
- Adzuna's terms rule it out as a commercial source for a paid report without a licence. Apify and Bright Data scraping of LinkedIn/Indeed is cheap but carries terms-of-service risk (general knowledge, not verified in this research).

### Gaps
- Current TheirStack self-serve plan grid (the $59 vs $98 vs $109 entry-price conflict) could not be confirmed, because the pricing page fetch was blocked.
- No public pricing was found for LinkUp or Revelio direct. Lightcast pricing is a 2022 client document and may not reflect current or commercial (non-economic-development) pricing.
- All freshness and coverage figures are vendor claims. No independent benchmark of recall or freshness by country or niche (e.g., UK niche sectors) was found.
- JobsPikr credit counts for the Starter and Growth plans were not found.

## Q2. Sales-intelligence platforms with hiring / job-posting signals: which include job-posting filters, and at what plan price

### Takeaway
Of the major sales-intelligence tools, **Apollo (from $49–59/user/month) and LinkedIn Sales Navigator (from about $90–120/month)** are the two that give a small agency a native "companies with open job postings" filter together with contacts in one self-serve product. Clay can orchestrate job-posting signals from third parties, but its self-serve plans start at $167–185/month. ZoomInfo and Cognism have hiring or intent signals but cost five figures a year and are sold by quote. Lusha and Seamless.AI put intent and job-change features behind higher tiers or add-ons. Crunchbase ($49–99/month) has "growth signals", but its headcount and hiring data reportedly lag.

### Inferences
- For a small agency, the realistic self-serve substitutes are **Apollo Basic/Professional** and **Sales Navigator Core**. - Sales Navigator's Job Opportunities filter only sees jobs posted on LinkedIn. Apollo's job-postings data source and freshness are not documented in anything found. - Clay is the most powerful DIY option (it can chain TheirStack/PredictLeads jobs with contact waterfalls and dedupe logic), but at $167–185/month plus the learning curve it is closer to what the solo founder would use to *produce* the report than what a small agency would adopt itself.

## Q3. How commoditised is the raw data? Can a small agency get the same thing themselves for under $100/month?

### Takeaway
The raw ingredients are highly commoditised and cheap. Job postings cost about $0.10–$2.50 per 1,000 records via scrapers and datasets, or are free in limited tiers (TheirStack, Techmap, PredictLeads). Contacts are available from $0 (Apollo free, limited credits) or $49–59/month (Apollo Basic). Recruitment-specific job-lead feeds already sell for £30–£250/month. So a small agency **can technically assemble a comparable signal stack for under $100/month**. What they cannot get at that price is the labour: niche curation, ghost-job and duplicate filtering, week-over-week novelty, and matching to the right decision-maker.

### Cited Findings
- Scraped LinkedIn jobs cost $0.10–$1.00 per 1,000 results on Apify marketplace actors (e.g., [supreme_coder $0.1/1K](https://apify.com/supreme_coder/linkedin-jobs-scraper), [northbell $0.50/1K](https://apify.com/northbell/linkedin-jobs-fast-scraper/examples/1000-jobs-for-fifty-cents)) [marketplace], and Apify's free tier includes $5 of credit per month — [Apify pricing](https://apify.com/pricing) [vendor]
- Bright Data LinkedIn/Indeed job datasets: $250 per 100K records (about $2.50/1K) — [Bright Data LinkedIn Jobs](https://brightdata.com/products/datasets/linkedin/jobs) [vendor]
- Free tiers: TheirStack 50 company + 200 API credits/month — [TheirStack pricing](https://theirstack.com/en/pricing) [vendor]; Techmap 1,000 jobs/month free — [Techmap pricing](https://jobdatafeeds.com/pricing) [vendor]; PredictLeads 100 free API credits/month, each call returning up to 1,000 records — [PredictLeads pricing](https://predictleads.com/pricing) [vendor]
- Apollo Basic is $49/user/month annual or $59 monthly — [Landbase Apollo pricing](https://www.landbase.com/blog/apollo-pricing) [competitor-authored]
- Apollo free-plan limits are reported inconsistently. One account gives 1,200 credits/year (100/month); another says 10,000 email credits/month on a corporate domain but only 100/month on a personal Gmail; 5 mobile credits/month; 10 export credits/month; 25 records per export; no rollover — [Alex Berman: Apollo free credits](https://alexberman.com/apollo-io-free-credits) [3rd-party]; [Scrupp: Apollo free plan export limit](https://scrupp.com/blog/apollo-io-free-plan-export-limit-workaround) [competitor-authored]. Exact attribution of each figure among these results was not verified.
- Sales Navigator Core alone is ~$89.99/month annual or $119.99 monthly — [Cleanlist: Sales Navigator pricing](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-05-08-linkedin-sales-navigator-pricing-guide) [competitor-authored]
- Google Alerts is free and can be pointed at job-related keywords — [Indeed Canada: Google Alerts for jobs](https://ca.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/google-alert-for-jobs) [3rd-party]; [MakeUseOf: Google Alerts in job search](https://www.makeuseof.com/use-google-alerts-in-job-search/) [3rd-party]
- **Recruitment-specific job-lead feeds already exist at low prices** (details in Q4): Spacewalk Job Radar £30/month — [Spacewalk Job Radar](https://www.gospacewalk.com/job-radar) [vendor]; Vacancysoft from £125/month — [Vacancysoft](https://vacancysoft.com/) [vendor]
- Data quality in the cheap contact layer is contested. More than 500 of Apollo's 9,000+ G2 reviews reportedly mention inaccurate or outdated data, with export bounce rates of 15–25% reported — [Salesforge: Apollo review](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/apollo-io-review) [competitor-authored]; [Amplemarket: what Apollo does](https://www.amplemarket.com/blog/what-does-apollo-really-do) [competitor-authored]. Apollo itself claims 97% email accuracy (same sources).

### Inferences
- **An illustrative DIY stack under $100/month** (my construction, not a sourced bundle): Apollo Basic ($49–59) for job-postings filters and contacts, plus free LinkedIn job search, TheirStack or Techmap free tier, and Google Alerts. That comes to about $49–59/month. Adding Spacewalk Job Radar (£30, about $40) still lands near $90–100. So price alone is not a moat for a weekly report. Anything priced well above what these tools cost has to be justified by time saved and quality.
- The data is commoditised; the **work** is not. A DIY user still has to (a) define niche-specific title and keyword searches across sources, (b) dedupe the same job across boards and across weeks, (c) weed out stale or ghost postings, (d) identify the right hiring manager, not the internal recruiter, and (e) verify emails. That is recurring weekly effort that small agencies may not do consistently. No source quantifying that time was found (see Gaps).
- Sales Navigator on its own busts a $100 budget on monthly billing and has no email data. The cheapest "filter + contacts" combination is Apollo.

### Gaps
- No survey or benchmark was found on how many hours per week recruiters spend on job-lead research, or what share of small agencies already use Apollo or Sales Navigator for business development. Such data would help quantify the "done-for-you" value.
- Could not verify whether Google Search's job-listing feature ("Google for Jobs") currently offers email alerts. The "Google Careers" alert help pages found apply to jobs at Google itself, not the general job search, so they were not used.
- Could not verify whether Apollo's free plan includes the job-postings filter (see Q2 conflict).

## Q4. What gaps could a curated, niche-specific weekly report fill (curation, freshness filtering, dedupe/"never twice", decision-maker matching, done-for-you)?

### Takeaway
Generic data vendors and sales-intelligence tools leave curation, ghost-job filtering, novelty across weeks and hiring-manager matching to the user. However, **a set of recruitment-specific "job leads + hiring-manager contacts" products already targets exactly this buyer**: Vacancysoft, Spacewalk Job Radar, Vente AI, JobGrabber, Agency Leads, and Oppora. So the defensible gap is narrower than "curation + contacts". The defensible part is niche depth, human-verified signal quality (real, fresh, not agency-posted, not ghost), guaranteed no-repeat delivery, and a finished short-list that needs no software. The data itself is not the gap.

### Cited Findings

**Recruitment-specific substitutes (closest competitors to the report format)**
- **Vacancysoft** (UK labour-market data publisher for the recruitment industry): subscriptions "from £125 per month", with a single-user licence for specialised services from £250/month; pay per data package; "only relevant vacancies from the last 24 hours in your inbox"; no limit on users — [Vacancysoft home](https://vacancysoft.com/) [vendor]; [Vacancysoft Vacancy Tracker](https://vacancysoft.com/vacancy-tracker/) [vendor]; [Vacancysoft about](https://vacancysoft.com/about-us/) [vendor]
- **Spacewalk Job Radar**: £30/month, 7-day free trial, no card needed; streams "real, active vacancies direct from employer career pages every 2 hours"; positions itself as a Vacancysoft alternative that aggregates 8 major job boards and appends decision-maker contact details — [Spacewalk Job Radar](https://www.gospacewalk.com/job-radar) [vendor]; [Spacewalk blog: Meet Job Radar](https://www.gospacewalk.com/blog/near-real-time-job-listings-for-recruiters-meet-job-radar) [vendor]
- **Vente AI** (London; raised £500k): "40M+ job leads" per month from 200+ sources, "hiring stress" scoring, spec-CV matching, finds hiring managers' email, mobile and LinkedIn, and pushes opportunities into JobAdder daily; Starter/Scale/Growth/Enterprise plans with a 12-month minimum; prices not captured — [Vente AI](https://www.vente.ai/) [vendor]; [Vente AI pricing](https://www.vente.ai/pricing) [vendor]; [JobAdder: Vente integration](https://jobadder.com/integration/vente-ai/) [partner]; [UKTN funding news](https://www.uktech.news/ai/vente-ai-recruitment-raises-500k-in-six-months-20241128) [press]
- **JobGrabber (eGrabber)**: scans job boards to find companies actively hiring, "with decision-maker contacts included"; used by staffing agencies in the USA, UK, Canada and Mexico; free trial; price not found — [eGrabber JobGrabber](https://www.egrabber.com/jobgrabber/) [vendor]
- **Agency Leads**: 229K+ verified staffing leads with job requirements and hiring-manager contacts, "live leads updated daily", 500 exports/month, email notifications; price by demo. - **Oppora.ai**: an AI system for agencies to "find hiring companies, identify decision-makers, verify contacts, personalize outreach… and book meetings" — [Oppora blog](https://oppora.ai/blog/best-tool-for-recruitment-agency-business-development/) [vendor]

**What generic tools leave undone (evidence for curation and freshness gaps)**
- Ghost and stale postings are widespread. Greenhouse reported about 18–22% of its postings were ghost jobs ([MarketBeat, Jan 2025: "~20% of its postings were ghost jobs"](https://www.marketbeat.com/articles/more-soul-crushing-than-ever-popular-hiring-platform-finds-around-20-of-its-postings-were-ghost-jobs-2025-01-13) [3rd-party press]). "1 in 3 US job listings go nowhere" — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/us-job-listings-go-nowhere-creating-a-ghost-job-economy/805448/) [3rd-party press]. A report claiming 27.4% of US LinkedIn listings are likely ghost jobs (ResumeUp.AI analysis) — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/millions-of-job-postings-are-for-ghost-jobs-study/499415) [3rd-party press; attribution from search summary]. Also the subject of a Congressional Research Service product — [Congress.gov CRS: "Ghost" Job Postings](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12977) [government; title only seen]
- Only some vendors dedupe across sources: TheirStack ("the same job posted on several platforms counts once") — [TheirStack Our Data](https://theirstack.com/en/our-data) [vendor]; Coresignal's multi-source API merges duplicates — [Coresignal Jobs API](https://coresignal.com/solutions/jobs-data-api/) [vendor]. - Freshness varies widely by source: TheirStack claims 73% same-day discovery ([TheirStack Our Data](https://theirstack.com/en/our-data)) [vendor]; PredictLeads updates every 36 hours ([PredictLeads Job Openings](https://predictleads.com/job_listings)) [vendor]; Job Radar every 2 hours ([Spacewalk](https://www.gospacewalk.com/job-radar)) [vendor]; ZoomInfo Scoops reportedly "lag behind job boards by days or weeks" ([Origami](https://origami.chat/blog/find-ceos-hiring-marketing-sales-developer-roles)) [competitor-authored, unverified attribution]; Crunchbase headcount data "lags" ([SyncGTM](https://syncgtm.com/blog/crunchbase-pro-review-2026)) [competitor-authored]
- Contact accuracy in the cheapest tools is contested (Apollo: 15–25% reported bounce rates; 500+ G2 reviews citing inaccurate data) — [Salesforge Apollo review](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/apollo-io-review) [competitor-authored]
- Recruiter-fit issues: Glozo argues Apollo's data "is framed around the assumption that the person you're contacting is a prospect", which suits client business development more than candidate sourcing — [Glozo: Apollo for recruiters](https://www.glozo.com/blog/apollo-for-recruiters) [competitor-authored]
- Existing "job lead" products carry commitment and complexity friction. Vente requires a 12-month minimum, and on monthly-paid accounts credits don't roll over ([Vente pricing](https://www.vente.ai/pricing)) [vendor]. Clay needs workflow building across Data Credits and Actions ([Salesforge: Clay pricing](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/clay-pricing)) [competitor-authored]. ZoomInfo and Cognism are annual contracts in the tens of thousands of dollars ([Factors.ai ZoomInfo](https://www.factors.ai/blog/zoominfo-pricing); [Factors.ai Cognism](https://www.factors.ai/blog/cognism-pricing)) [competitor-authored]

### Inferences
- **Gaps a weekly curated report can credibly own:**
  1. **Niche depth.** Generic tools filter by job title and keyword. A curated report can apply niche judgement (e.g., "senior embedded engineers at Series A–C medtech in the North West") and exclude noise: agency-posted roles, evergreen or ghost reqs, roles the client fills in-house.
  2. **Signal quality over volume.** Filtering out ghost and stale postings (18–33% of listings by the estimates above), reposts and duplicates is manual or semi-manual work that the self-serve tools leave to the user.
  3. **"Never twice" / week-over-week novelty.** Vendors dedupe *across sources* (TheirStack, Coresignal), but no source found describes guaranteed non-repetition *across deliveries* for a customer. TheirStack's alerts on "new matches" partly cover this. A report can promise "only companies you haven't seen before" as an explicit feature. (This is an inference: absence of evidence, not confirmed absence.)
  4. **Decision-maker matching.** Joining a posting to the actual hiring manager or department head, not HR or the internal TA team, and verifying the email is where cheap contact data is weakest (reported bounce rates of 15–25%).
  5. **Done-for-you, no software.** Every alternative except Vacancysoft's inbox digest and Job Radar's feed needs someone to log in, build searches and export. A PDF/CSV in the inbox with a short "why now" note per company is a different product.
- **Competitive threat:** Vacancysoft (£125+/month), Job Radar (£30/month) and Vente (annual) already deliver recruitment-specific vacancy leads, some with contacts. The report's price and positioning need to sit against these, not against ZoomInfo. The clearest room is below Vente/Vacancysoft on commitment (monthly, cancel anytime) and above Job Radar on curation depth and contact quality.
- **Upstream sourcing option:** TheirStack or Techmap (API/free tiers) plus Apollo for contacts would let a solo founder produce the report at low data cost. The margin then depends on curation time per niche, not on data spend.

### Gaps
- Vente AI plan prices, JobGrabber prices and Agency Leads' exact plan prices were not found (pricing pages were blocked or behind a demo).
- No independent reviews (G2/Capterra ratings or user complaints) of Vacancysoft, Job Radar, Vente or JobGrabber were gathered, so their real-world data quality and churn are unknown.
- No source was found that measures how often the same company recurs week to week in generic job alerts. That would quantify the value of a "never twice" feature.
- Ghost-job estimates vary by method and source (18% to about 33%). Most are from job-seeker-oriented or resume-tool vendors and should be treated as indicative, not definitive.
